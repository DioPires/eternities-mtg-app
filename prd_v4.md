## 1. Summary

Eternities v4 is a free desktop application in which you walk the planes of the Magic: The Gathering multiverse as a planeswalker and look at the cards that were first printed there. You start in the hub, a dark space with one portal per plane. You step through a portal and arrive on a world: flat terrain built for that plane, with its own sky, light, weather, water, vegetation or metal or stone, ambient sound and music. The cards of that plane stand on the terrain as flat exhibits, laid out by a law you can learn: a trail runs through the plane's history, each era of that history is a site with a reading stone, and the cards of an era stand in small groups beside the site's winding path. On a hero world the regions you cross are the places the plane's lore names; on a kit world the trail runs through the plane's sets in release order. Walk up to a card and the view changes: the card fills the frame, the world stays behind it, and a panel lists every printing to click through. Leave the view and keep walking. A travel menu opens a portal to any card by name.

It is a place you visit, not a game you play. There are no objectives, no score, no combat, no inventory, no progression and no winning state. Everything else about it, the environments, the third-person avatar, the lighting, the sky, the props, the sound and the feel of moving, is built to the standard of a modern 3D game.

Everything visible is still derived from data. The Python pipeline of v3 keeps every inclusion rule, the first-printing rule and the plane assignment. It gains one curated table for every plane (Appendix C: one theme row per plane) and, for each of the twenty hero entries, three curated lore tables (Appendix F: regions, eras, keywords) written in our own words from named sources, and emits a second data pack for the engine. The renderer moves from the browser to Unity 6 with the High Definition Render Pipeline. The shipped web application stays deployed as the web edition, refreshed with the same data, and remains the screen-reader path to the catalogue.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

### 2.1 Goals

1. **A place, not a game.** Eternities is an explorable gallery. Every feature must pass the test "would this still make sense in a museum?" Walking, looking, reading, travelling and listening pass. Points, quests, timers, rewards, enemies, unlocks and achievements fail. The Fan Content Policy forbids Wizards' IP in games or game components, free or not, and privacy of distribution is no exemption. This document cannot settle whether a walkable 3D gallery with a named avatar is a game in the policy's sense; section 10 risk 1 and open question 11.1 make that an owner decision, with a legal read, before the build is given to anyone outside the project, friends included. Until then every design choice is made to keep the product on the gallery side of the line.
2. **Game-grade feel.** Environments, avatar, lighting, sky, weather, water, props, sound and movement are built to the standard of a current 3D game. A plane with rivers and lakes shows and sounds like water; a plane of lava and ash shows and sounds like fire. This is the product's reason to exist after v3's "mesmerising by default", and it is a goal, not a polish item.
3. **Planes differ, in stages.** At every release a visitor can tell which **kit** they are on without reading a name, and two worlds on the same kit differ by their theme rows (sky, light, palette, weather, water, sound). The twenty largest entries of the roster (92.0 percent of all cards, including the Blind Eternities) are **hero entries**: each is laid out from its own lore tables (regions, eras, keywords; Appendix F), so its geography is the lore's and its trail is its history, and each additionally gets hand-built hero set-pieces that make it unmistakable. By the owner's decision of 2026-10-12, all twenty table sets are written and accepted before the first distribution, and every hero world is built from its tables (4.16.5, 8.12.7). Set-pieces still ship in stages, Dominaria first (8.12.4), and a hero entry without its set-piece is held to the kit standard for its set-piece alone. Pockets are identical by design: they hold nothing but a name.
4. **The picture is the data.** Where a card stands, which site and cluster it belongs to, where the trail runs, how large a world is, which portals exist: all derived from real card data, the curated tables and the fixed seed by one deterministic, terminating allocator (5.4). Nothing decorative is fabricated where a data-driven form is possible. The curated inputs to the picture are Appendix C and, on hero entries, the three lore tables of Appendix F; the tables name places, eras and sources, never positions.
5. **Findable.** Any card, plane or set is reachable by search and by the travel menu without walking. Selecting a destination moves the avatar there through a portal without the visitor steering; the bench times it for same-world and cross-world destinations (7.2).
6. **Maintainable by one person.** Curated inputs are Appendices A, B, C and F, `overrides.json`, `avatar-overrides.json` and the hero volumes files. The pipeline fails loudly on anything it cannot place. Adding a set is one Appendix B row and one run, plus, on a hero entry, its code in an era row of that entry's table (a set with first printings and no era fails the build, 5.4.3); adding a plane is one row in each appendix. Hero set-pieces and avatars are the owner's own work and are additive to a working kit.
7. **Free, private, unofficial, compliant.** No accounts, no telemetry, no server. The Fan Content Policy notice is on the start screen. Scryfall is credited. Card images are shown as complete cards, flat, undistorted, untinted, with the artist line the card itself carries and a text credit beside it.

### 2.2 Non-goals (v4)

1. Any game mechanic: score, quests, objectives, timers, combat, inventory, collection meters, progression, unlocks, achievements, leaderboards, saving "progress" beyond settings and the recently-visited list.
2. Multiplayer or any online component beyond fetching images.
3. A web edition of v4. The v3 web application stays deployed and data-refreshed (8.11). A Unity web export is possible later and is not a goal.
4. Mobile, tablet, touch, VR.
5. Voice acting, licensed Wizards music or video, Wizards logos, set symbols, the planeswalker symbol or mana symbols as authored assets.
6. Steam or any store that frames the product as a game.
7. Universes Beyond and Secret Lair content, *Un*-sets, digital-only sets, tokens, emblems, art series, promos, oversized cards (as v3 2.2.3 and 2.2.4).
8. Gameplay data: prices, format legality, rulings, decklists, collection tracking.
9. Lore content beyond the reading layer of 5.13: no character dialogue, no story text quoted from Wizards, no prose on kit worlds beyond set names and years. *Amendment 4: the first print excluded all lore text; the owner reversed that for the reading stones of hero worlds on 2026-10-12 (5.13, Appendix E.10).*
10. Automatic data refresh; the pipeline runs by hand.
11. Localisation; English only.
12. More than one avatar in v1 (Appendix D); no avatar selection screen in v1.
13. A second render pipeline (URP) in v1 (8.1.1).
14. Basic-land signposts, generative music, runtime texture compression, and screen-reader support in the native build. Each is recorded in section 11 with the reason it waits. *Amendment 4: per-plane wedge dressing left this list; the region tables do that work (5.6.3, 11.9).*

### 3.2 Added invariants

6. Every plane in Appendix A is reachable from the hub through exactly one portal, the Blind Eternities included: 88 portals.
7. Every included card has exactly one stand, in the site that 5.4.3 assigns it (its era's site, the regional site of its keyword's region, or its set's site), in the world of its plane.
8. Stand positions, region cells, site centres, blobs, site paths, clusters, the trail, aprons, corridors, pads, stones, world size and portal placement are a pure function of the input data, the appendices, the lore tables, the hero volumes files and the fixed seed, computed by the allocator of 5.4 in integer millimetres. A refresh may move them; a release never changes them under the user. Stands are fixed once the pack is emitted; no runtime system moves a stand.
9. **Exclusions by geometry type, checked on the final (jittered) positions.** No two stands are closer than 2.5 m. A stand's footprint and apron intersect nothing else: not a path, not a pad, not a stone, not a corridor of another stand, not the plaza, not a hero volume, not another stand's footprint or apron. A corridor intersects no footprint or apron of another stand, no stone, no hero volume and no plaza, and ends on its site's path at a vertex within 6 m of its cluster's slot along the path; corridors may cross each other and may cross a pad's edge. A pad lies on the path network by design (a site pad at its path's head, where its approach meets the path; a hub portal pad on the hub's ring) or inside the plaza (the return portal's pad), and intersects no footprint, apron, hero volume or other pad. A stone stands beside its pad's approach (5.4.8) or at a lattice point inside a cluster (a chapter stone, 5.4.10) by design and intersects nothing else: no footprint, apron, corridor, path, approach, pad, hero volume or other stone. A hero volume may overlap the plaza but not the arrival point, the return pad, the trail, the plaza approach, any site path, any approach or any site's bounding disc (5.4.6). Every stand lies inside its site's blob and within the cluster disc of its cluster, and a cluster holds at most eight stands (3.1). Every stand faces its path vertex: its yaw lies within 0.1° of the direction from its emitted position to that vertex. Both are read as counts of thousandths of a degree: the yaw's count and the count that 5.4.18 (7) finds for the vector from the emitted position to the vertex differ by at most 100, the short way round the circle. Rounding the two points moves that direction by less than 0.03°, because a stand's centre lies at least 3.15 m from its path vertex (half the footprint's depth, the apron's depth and the path's half width). Every point of a site's path, pad, approach and stone lies within b_s of the site's centre c_s (5.4.8). That test reads the true circle, by squared distance; where the bounding disc meets another shape (5.4.6, and a hero volume above), it is the 72-gon of 5.4.18 (13), which lies inside the circle up to the trig table's rounding (at most 5 × 10⁻¹⁰ of the radius, under 0.001 mm for a radius under 2 km). The independent checker applies exactly these rules to the emitted geometry, lists violations in the report, and the build fails on any. It does not test what the constants fix whatever the data, such as the plaza approach's 10 m length and the return pad's place at (0, −6,500) mm: the fixtures pin those byte for byte (5.4.14). Nor does it test which angle 5.4.11 chose for the plaza approach, because that is a choice and not a property of the result. It tests the result: no hero volume meets the plaza approach (above), and the network is connected (invariant 10). The fixtures pin the choice.
10. The path network is connected: the trail reaches every site's pad from the plaza's edge path, every site path begins at its pad, every corridor reaches its site's path; and the kit may not block a path or a corridor except with a ford or bridge prefab. The trail visits the sites in site order (5.4.11): in leg order, each leg ends at the approach point of the next era site (hero world) or set site (kit world), and each spur ends at the approach point of the next regional site. The checker asserts connectivity and that order on the emitted network.
11. In an era site, stands run in chapter order along the path from the pad outward **by cluster**: a stand in a lower slot holds an earlier or the same chapter as any stand in a higher slot, and every chapter after the first has its chapter stone, at a lattice point inside the disc of the cluster that holds the chapter's first stand or of the site's cluster before that one (the stone can take the last unit of the earlier cluster's capacity, 5.4.10); on a hero world, every stand whose card has a selected keyword (5.4.3) stands in that keyword's region, and every other stand of a hero world stands in its era's region. The checker asserts both on the emitted geometry against the tables. Within a cluster the acceptance order also holds the chapter order (5.4.10), but the files do not show it, because 4.13.3 sorts a cluster's records by `oracle_id`; so the checker asserts the chapter order by cluster only. *(Erratum 5, 2026-10-14, DEC-1096, from the review of PR #137 on DEC-1091, notes N5 to N13: the rules on a cluster's eight stands and a stand's yaw (9), the trail's order (10) and a chapter stone's cluster (11) are new, with the two-vertex path of 5.4.10. Leg 1's pull request 2 adds them to both checkers; until it merges, the pipeline's checker does not assert them. The CEO ruled that a fact that the data can break gains a rule, and a fact that the constants fix does not, because the fixtures already pin it.)*

### 3.3 Vocabulary

One term per concept throughout this document and the codebase. These replace the v3 star vocabulary; v3's data terms (plane, card, printing, first printing) are unchanged.

- **plane** for the domain object, **world** for its rendered form; never "level", "map" (except for the HUD map), "galaxy" or "setting".
- **hub** for the arrival space; never "lobby" or "menu".
- **stand** for a card's exhibit; **image surface** for the part that shows the card; never "pedestal" or "card object". **Panel** is used for exactly one thing: the card view's printings panel (5.10.3).
- **plaza**, **region**, **era**, **keyword**, **site**, **cluster**, **chapter**, **trail**, **path**, **corridor**, **pad**, **stone** (reading stone, waystone, marker stone, chapter stone), **portal**, **apron**, **hero volume**, **lore tables** as defined above. Never **wedge**, **band** or **ring**: Amendment 4 retired them, and the hub's portal **ring** (5.3.2) is the one remaining use of that word.
- **avatar** for the planeswalker the visitor moves as; never "character" or "player model".
- **explore** for the third-person walking mode, **card view** for the focused mode, **map** for the top-down HUD overlay.
- **travel** for any movement that is not walking: portals, waystones, the travel menu.
- **kit** for a procedural biome, **theme row** for a plane's Appendix C row, **hero set-piece** for hand-built geometry on a hero entry.
- **internal evaluation** for use by the owner and the agents; **distribution** for giving the build to anyone else, friends included.

### 4.12 Appendix C: plane theme table

### 4.15 Scryfall on a native client

1. At runtime the application contacts only Scryfall's image CDN, which is two hostnames: `cards.scryfall.io` for card faces and `backs.scryfall.io` for the card back (5.10.2). Every other mention of "Scryfall's image CDN" in this document (7.5.1, 8.10, 9.1.6, Appendix E.8) means these two hostnames, and the loader refuses a redirect to any other host. It never calls the Scryfall API (`api.scryfall.com`); every card fact it shows is in the data pack. The pipeline, as in v3, fetches bulk data at build time. *Amendment 2 (2026-10-10, DEC-952): the first print said "exactly one host"; the card back is served from a second hostname, and a loader built to one shows no card back.*
2. Images are fetched at the size the view needs: `normal` (488 × 680) for stands, `large` (672 × 936) for card view. `small` and `art_crop` are not used. Images are never bundled, mirrored or bulk-downloaded. Scryfall's Imagery page (read 2026-10-10) lists WEBP sizes `grid` and `display` as replacing `normal` and `large` and announces no retirement, so the JPG sizes stay in use; the loader keeps each size's name and file extension in one table, and the refresh runbook's terms re-read includes that page. *(Amendment 2 note.)*
3. An on-disk cache under the OS application-support directory holds fetched images, capped (default 2 GB, least recently used eviction, user-adjustable). Clearing it is a settings action. The cache is filled only by images a view requested (5.5.5, 5.10.2): the hub's world preload (5.3.3) fetches no card image, nothing else prefetches, warms or crawls ahead of the visitor, and the cache is never exported, shared or served to another device. Each entry is keyed on the full image URL including its query string, and an entry older than the origin's `max-age` is treated as absent. *Amendment 2 (2026-10-10, DEC-952): the fill rule, not the cap, is what makes this a cache and not a mirror; 2 GB would hold about three quarters of the catalogue at `normal`.*
4. Requests carry an identifying `User-Agent` (`Eternities/<version> (+<repository URL>)`) and `Accept: image/*`; the image-path tests assert the outgoing `User-Agent`, because an engine's HTTP stack sends its own default unless one is set, and assert that the URL builder emits nothing outside the fixed path pattern over a validated UUID, because on the image hostnames a malformed path draws a 400 (probed 2026-10-10) and a 400 pauses every request. One concurrency limit applies: 6 in flight, counted across both hostnames. A 5xx response retries that URL with exponential backoff (1 s, 4 s, 16 s) and then leaves it for the session. A network failure retries the same way, but a URL left after network failures (every attempt a network failure; one 5xx among them leaves it for the session) becomes eligible again once any image request succeeds, so a dropped connection does not pin stands on their swatches until restart. While any URL is left after network failures, no pause is in force and no image request is in flight, the loader retries one such URL every 30 s, so a visitor who stands still gets images back without doing anything. A 429, a 403 or a 400 from either hostname pauses every image request, not only that URL, for at least 30 s (longer if `Retry-After` says so), queues that URL again, doubles the pause as served on each repeat (the next pause is the longer of twice the last and its own `Retry-After`), and resumes with one request in flight until a request succeeds, then returns to six. A 429, 403 or 400 that arrives while a pause is in force, including from a request already in flight when the pause began, belongs to that pause and is not a repeat; its URL is queued again. The third such pause in a session is not served: the loader stops fetching for the rest of the session and stands show their swatches (an image already resident or on disk still shows, 7.4.3). A redirect the loader refuses (4.15.1) is final for that URL for the session. Any other 4xx is final for that URL for the session. There is never a retry storm. The web edition's `docs/scryfall-policy.md` was re-verified for a desktop client on 2026-10-10 (DEC-952, §11 of that document), a 9.5 prerequisite. *Amendment 2 (2026-10-10, DEC-952): the first print backed off per URL, with retries at 1 s, 5 s and 21 s that all fall inside the 30 s for which Scryfall limits access after a 429, and treated a 403 as a missing image; a missing image is a 404.* *Amendment 2 erratum (2026-10-10, DEC-954 review notes N2 and N5 to N8): 400 joined the pause, because Scryfall's Access FAQ puts 400 beside 403 for its static files and a 400 is a statement about the client's request, not about one image; the pause, repeat and probe readings above are the ones the slice builds; a network failure no longer leaves a URL for the session.* *Amendment 2 erratum 2 (2026-10-10, DEC-955 review notes N10 to N12): the idle retry every 30 s, so a visitor who stands still recovers from a network drop; the URL-builder test, because on the image hostnames a 400 answers a malformed path (the first erratum's reason, rejected headers, describes the API host); and four readings the sentence left open are now in its text: the URL that drew the pause is queued again, the pause doubles as served, only a URL whose every attempt was a network failure returns, and a resident image stays after the stop.*
5. **Attribution.** Only complete card images are shown, and a complete card carries its artist line in the printed frame. In addition, card view shows the artist as text beside the printings panel (5.10.3) and the stand's hover label shows the artist (5.5.3). A screenshot therefore always contains the printed credit of every visible card image; a stand showing its swatch shows no image and needs no credit. The image surface, on a stand and in card view, takes its aspect from the image's pixel dimensions (488 × 680 and 672 × 936, about 5:7), never a nominal ratio, so the image is not stretched. *(Amendment 2 note: Scryfall's "do not distort, skew, or stretch" guideline; a 5:7 surface would stretch a `normal` image by 0.5 percent.)*

### 4.16 Lore tables (hero entries)

*Amendment 4 (2026-10-12, owner decision on DEC-950): by the owner's choice, every hero entry is laid out from three curated lore tables, and all twenty table sets are accepted before the first distribution. Appendix F has the schema and the rules in full; this subsection is the data contract.*

1. **Source form.** One directory per hero entry, `pipeline/data/lore/<slug>/`, with three JSON files: `regions.json` (the plane's places: 4 to 12 rows, each with a kit or variant, a rough position, a weight, its water, one sourced sentence and its sources), `eras.json` (the plane's history in lore order: 3 to 16 rows, or 3 to 24 for the Blind Eternities' product table, each with its sets, its region, its headline cards, one to three paragraphs whose every sentence carries its source, and the owner's acceptance record), and `keywords.json` (words of the lore that name a region). JSON, not YAML: the pipeline validates and resolves the tables and emits `lore/<slug>.json` (4.16.3) in the same syntax, so one parser serves both sides and the engine never reads the source tables. The Appendix C.1 row of a hero entry names the directory in its `lore` field.
2. **Validation** (every pipeline run, failing the build with the row named): ids are unique slugs; anchors are integer pairs in thousandths inside the unit disc (u² + v² ≤ 1,000,000) and pairwise at least 150 apart; weights are integers in thousandths in [500, 2000]; no number in the three files is a fraction (F.0); at most three distinct kits across an entry's regions (11.18); every region's kit, variant, water and ambient are values 4.12.2 and `kits.yaml` allow; every set with first printings on the plane appears in exactly one era, and an era names at least one set; every era's region exists; every headline card name is first printed in one of the era's sets; one to three paragraphs per era, one to six sentences per paragraph, at most 40 words per sentence and 300 per era; **every sentence has a source** on the allowlist of F.4; the presentation lint of 9.1.6 passes on every sentence; every keyword is at least three characters and names an existing region; every file carries exactly the fields of Appendix F.0 with their types; the cross-file identity checks of F.0 pass; for a product table, each era's sets lie in its region's family list, the families cover the plane's set codes exactly once, and the keyword table is empty; every source is validated by parsing its locator (F.4); and an `accepted` record, when present, has exactly the fields and types of F.0. Whether its digest matches the files is the **acceptance status** of F.2 (accepted, stale or absent), which every run reports and only `--distribution` enforces (4.16.5); it is never a validation failure, so a validating table set with a stale record still builds in development.
3. **Emission.** The resolved tables are emitted into the engine pack as `lore/<slug>.json` (4.13.2): eras with their text, years, region, sets, headline stand indices and sources; regions with their name, sentence and sources; the keyword list. The region cells and the trail are geometry and live in `worlds/<slug>.json` (4.13.5). The engine never carries a second copy of a table.
4. **Retained field.** The pipeline retains Scryfall's `story_spotlight` flag per printing (an amendment to v3 4.8). 5.13.2 uses it for an era's headline cards. A flagged printing counts only for a card that stands on the entry's plane, and only when its set is the card's first set (5.13.2). *(Erratum 2, 2026-10-13, DEC-1014.)*
5. **The distribution flag.** A tagged build runs the pipeline with `--distribution`. Under it, every `hero: true` row (exactly the twenty of Appendix C.1, asserted) must have a lore directory that validates and whose `eras.json` carries an `accepted` record whose digest matches the files (F.2); otherwise the build fails naming the entry. The build script refuses to package a distribution archive from a pack whose manifest does not record `distribution: true`, so the check runs at pack build and again at packaging (8.9.1). Without the flag (a development build), a hero entry whose tables validate is laid out from them whatever their acceptance status, and a stale or absent `accepted` record is reported, not fatal; a hero entry with no lore directory is laid out by the kit law (the release-order reading of 5.4) and the report lists it under "hero entries without tables"; tables that fail validation fail the build in either mode. So development never waits on acceptance, and no distribution ships without it.
6. **Provenance.** Each sentence's source is one locator of the five forms of Appendix F.4: a page under `magic.wizards.com`, `scryfall.com` or `mtg.wiki`, validated by parsing the host; `card:<oracle_id>` for a card's name and type line; `printing:<scryfall_id>#front` or `#back` for a printing's printed flavour text. A source supports a sentence; it is never copied: the MTG Wiki's text is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (read 2026-10-12) and would bind ours, and Wizards' story text is theirs. The reviewer's check (5.13.4) is for support and for independent wording against the source the sentence names, and the visitor sees the sources in the reading stone's fold (5.13.1). One source per sentence makes the check possible and is not a licence determination (F.4).

## 5. The world

### 5.1 Principles

1. **Game-grade place.** The reference is a current third-person 3D game's environment art, not data visualisation. Real terrain, real water, real sky, real sound.
2. **The cards are exhibits.** Every card image is shown as a complete card, flat, upright, undistorted, untinted by lighting, exposure, fog or post-processing, and sharp. The world is lit; the cards are not. 8.4.4 names the rendering path that makes this true and the tests that check it.
3. **Never static.** Water flows, grass and trees move, clouds drift, particles rise, the avatar idles. Stillness happens only where the user asks for it (card view, reduced motion).
4. **One law for every world, read two ways.** Plaza, trail, sites, clusters, paths, pads and stones follow 5.4 on every world, so a visitor who has learned one world can read them all. On a hero world the trail runs through the plane's history in the lore's order and the regions are the lore's places (Appendix F); on a kit world the trail runs through the plane's sets in release order on one biome. Colour is read on the stand and in the HUD, never on the ground (5.5.1).
5. **Constrained freedom.** The avatar can go anywhere on the terrain and nowhere off it. World edges are natural (sea, cliffs, mist, a void) and never an invisible wall at a visible edge.

### 5.3 The hub

1. The application opens in the hub after the start screen. The hub is a compact void under a slow starfield, lit cold, on the `void` kit with its own hero set-piece: the **portal ring**.
2. **88 portals** stand on a ring of radius 56 m around the arrival point, 4.0 m apart centre to centre (circumference 352 m), one per plane in Appendix A including the Blind Eternities. Order: clockwise from north by the plane's first appearance year, ties by slug; empty planes last, alphabetical by slug. Each portal has a 3 m ring, a 0.5 m frame on each side (envelope 4 m, so neighbours touch at the frame and never overlap) and a 3 m pad in front of it toward the centre. Portal height scales with the log of the plane's card count in three steps; an empty plane's portal is small and dim but present (invariant 6). A waystone beside each pad shows the plane's name and card count.
3. **Preload.** At most one world preloads at a time: the one whose portal pad the avatar is nearest within 10 m. Changing the nearest portal cancels the previous preload after a 1 s hysteresis. Stepping into a portal whose world is not loaded uses the hold of 5.9.2.
4. The hub holds no stands. The Blind Eternities' 4,204 cards stand in the Blind Eternities' own world, reached through its portal like any plane; that world uses the `void` kit at world scale (Appendix C.1).
5. The hub has the travel menu (6.6), search and settings. The map in the hub shows the portal ring. Esc from any world returns to the hub through the return portal.

### 5.4 The world layout law and its allocator

*Amendment 4 (2026-10-12, owner decision on DEC-950 after the slice): this section replaces the first print's law in full. The first print's law, "colour is the wedge, time is the radius, each set is a site", produced a wheel that the owner judged a chart on the first slice (plan §13; DEC-953, question 3: no). Its machinery is kept: the global lattice, the jitter, the reservations, the independent checker, integer millimetres, the bounded loops and the named failure modes. Its geometry is withdrawn: there are no wedges, bands or rings, and colour is a cue on the stand (5.5.1), never ground. Draft 2 of the amendment (2026-10-12, after Codex round 5, plan §14.5) rewrote 5.4.1 to 5.4.15 and added 5.4.18. Draft 3 (2026-10-12, after Codex round 6, plan §14.5) made the pipeline's implementation the only allocator (8.3.3), replaced the two-implementation numerical contract with the reproducibility rules of 5.4.18, and corrected the procedure where round 6 showed it could fail on valid input: the approach (5.4.8, 5.4.11), the empty path and the degenerate cell (5.4.5, 5.4.8), chapter order (5.4.10), the corridor vertex (5.4.9) and the scale figures (5.4.15). Erratum 4 (2026-10-14, DEC-1040), after leg 1 ran the law on Dominaria's accepted tables, corrected the procedure where it failed on that data: the joined chord ends (5.4.1, 5.4.8), the path vertex's window (5.4.9, 5.4.10), the withdrawn cluster and one stand a lattice point (5.4.10), the redraw (5.4.10, 5.4.12) and the leg check (5.4.11); 5.4.15 gives the figures.*

The law a visitor learns: **the trail runs through the plane's history; each era is a site; the cards of an era stand in small groups beside the site's winding path; colour is on the stand, not on the ground.** On a hero world the eras and their places come from the plane's lore tables (Appendix F): the regions you cross are the lore's places and the trail visits the eras in the lore's order. On a kit world the "eras" are the plane's sets in release order and the place is one biome. The allocator below makes the law a terminating, deterministic procedure. **It is implemented once, in the pipeline (8.3.3); the engine reads its output and never computes a position.** 5.4.18 states the rules under which that one implementation is reproducible on every machine, the fixtures of 5.4.14 pin its output byte for byte, and an independent checker asserts the invariants of 3.2 on the emitted geometry. 5.4.15 states what has been computed so far and what has not.

**5.4.1 Constants.** All emitted lengths are integer millimetres and all emitted angles integer micro-degrees (5.4.18); the metre figures below are the stored integers divided by 1,000. Lattice pitch p = 3.0 m on the integer hexagonal lattice of 5.4.18 (nearest neighbours exactly 3.0 m apart); jitter radius j = 0.248 m, so any two stands are more than 2.5 m apart after rounding, because a lattice point holds at most one stand or stone (5.4.10, 5.4.18). Stand footprint 0.7 × 0.3 m; apron 1.0 m wide × 1.5 m deep on the side the image surface faces; corridor width 1.5 m. Path width 3 m (half width 1.5 m), for the trail, for every site path and for every approach. Pad: a disc of **radius 1.5 m**. Stone: a disc of **radius 0.5 m**. Plaza: a disc of radius 12 m at the world origin with an edge path of width 3 m outside it (outer radius 15 m) and a **plaza approach**, a straight 3 m wide strip of length 10 m from the edge path's outer boundary outward, where the trail attaches (5.4.11). Cluster: centre offset d = 6.0 m from the path's centreline; cluster disc radius ρ = 4.5 m; slot spacing along the path s = 9.0 m, sides alternating; size cap k drawn per slot from {3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8}. Site path: sweep spacing h = 21.0 m (= 2(d + ρ)); chord shortening 1.5 m at the head end of sweep 0, 15 m (4.5 m plus h ⁄ 2) at an end that a half-turn joins, and 4.5 m at the far end of the last sweep before any drop (5.4.8); half-turn radius h ⁄ 2; sweep wobble amplitude 3.0 m, wavelength drawn from [24 m, 40 m], tapered to zero over the 6 m nearest each chord end; a sweep shorter than s after shortening is dropped. **Approach:** a straight strip of width 3 m from the pad's centre outward along the site's head ray to the **approach point**, 10 m from the pad's centre (5.4.8); the trail attaches there (5.4.11). Site blob: base radius **r_s = max(9,000 mm, isqrt(K_site × n))** for a site of n stands, with **K_site = 11,459,156 mm²**, the integer nearest 36 m² ⁄ π (A_site = 36 m² of ground per stand; K_site is a defined constant of 5.4.18 (13), and π is never computed), so r_s is an integer; a widening replaces r_s by ⌊11 r_s ⁄ 10⌋; boundary deformation within ±20 percent (5.4.7); **site bounding radius b_s = ⌈6 r_s ⁄ 5⌉ + 15,000 mm** (1.2 r_s + 15 m rounded up to the millimetre), an integer, which contains the blob, the whole site path with its wobble and half-turns, the pad, the stone and the approach (5.4.8). Region: anchors inside the unit disc and pairwise at least 0.15 apart in table units; usable cell margin 10 m; site centre lattice pitch 10 m; minimum spacing 10 m between a site's bounding disc and every other site's bounding disc, the plaza's edge path and every hero volume. Trail: routed on a 5 m grid with obstacles expanded by 5 m (5.4.11); wobble amplitude 3.0 m, wavelength from [30 m, 50 m], tapered to zero over the 10 m nearest each end of a leg. World: initial radius R₀ (5.4.4), floor 30 m; widening step 10 percent (R becomes ⌈11 R ⁄ 10⌉ mm); at most 20 widenings per site; **redraw limit** 1, so a site is redrawn at most once at each of its radii (5.4.10); at most 20 outer passes. Prop clearance 1 m around paths, corridors, aprons, pads and stones; water buffer 3 m around the same. The world disc is a regular 72-gon and a blob is sampled at 72 vertices. PRNG: splitmix64, seeded with FNV-1a 64 of the string `eternities-v4|<plane slug>|<site key>` per site, followed by `|1` while the site's redraw count is 1 (5.4.10), and `eternities-v4|<plane slug>|world` for world-level draws; every draw is listed, in order, in the subsection that takes it, and a site's stream is reset to its seed whenever that site is recomputed (5.4.9); the world stream is reset at the start of every outer pass and every restart (5.4.12). Every decision is made on exact arithmetic with a 2 mm safety margin and every emitted coordinate is rounded once (5.4.18), so the independent checker, which tests the rounded geometry against the nominal figures of this subsection, finds what the allocator accepted.

**5.4.2 Sites and their order.** On a hero world: one **era site** per row of the era table, in the table's order; then one **regional site** per region that receives at least one card under the keyword rule of 5.4.3, in region-table order. On a kit world: one **set site** per set with first printings on the plane, in release order, ties by set code. The plaza is not a site. A site's key is `era:<id>`, `region:<id>` or `set:<code>`; the key seeds its PRNG. This order is the **site order** and the trail visits the sites in it (5.4.11). An era site whose every card stands at a regional site (5.4.3) is still a site, and the trail visits it; it has no cluster and no chapter stone, and its path ends at its second vertex (5.4.10).

**5.4.3 Assignment of cards to sites.** Kit world: every card goes to the site of its first-printing set. Hero world: (a) the card's **era** is the era whose `sets` list carries the card's first-printing set code; the table maps every set code with first printings on the plane exactly once, otherwise the build fails naming the set (Appendix F.2); (b) if the card's name on any face contains a **keyword** of the keyword table as a whole word (case-insensitive, with a word boundary at each end), the card's **selected keyword** is the first matching keyword in table order; if the selected keyword's region differs from the era's region, the card goes to the **regional site** of that region; otherwise, and when no keyword matches, the card goes to its era's site. The rule folds the keyword and the name by Unicode full case folding before it looks for the keyword in the name. A word boundary is the string's start or end, or a code point whose Unicode general category starts with Z (a separator) or P (punctuation). A letter, a digit, a combining mark, a symbol such as `+` and a control character are not boundaries. The folding and the categories are those of Unicode 15.1, the tables of the pipeline's Python 3.13 (`str.casefold` and `unicodedata`). The decoder's recomputation (4.13.3) reaches the same decisions. Where its runtime lacks those tables, a test runs that recomputation for every stand of every hero world in the pack that the player ships, and compares each card's site with the site that the stand's record names. On a product table (Appendix F.2) the keyword table is empty by validation, so every card stands in its family's region. Within an era site cards are ordered by **chapter**: the era's sets in release order (ties by code), then by `oracle_id` bytes; a chapter is empty when every card of its set went to a regional site, and an empty chapter has no stone and no stands. Within a regional site or a set site: by first-printing set release order, then by `oracle_id` bytes. The report lists every keyword assignment, every card whose name matches keywords of two regions, and every empty chapter.

**5.4.4 Initial radius.** With b_s each site's bounding radius (5.4.1) and b_max the largest of them, R₀ = max(30 m, isqrt(2 × Σ_s b_s²) + 25 m, 35 m + 2 b_max): the site bounding discs fill at most half the world disc; the plaza with its edge path (15 m) and one cell margin (10 m) are added; and the third term is a start for the largest site between the plaza's 10 m spacing and the 10 m cell margin (15 + 10 + 2 b_max + 10). It is not the least radius at which that site fits, because it leaves out three things: the 2 mm margin (5.4.18 (4)), the 10 m lattice of site centres (5.4.6) and the world polygon's inner radius, R cos 2.5°, about 0.999 R. No one-site world of the fixtures is laid out at R₀: Shandalar and Zhalfir take 3 passes, Vryn 2 and Rabiah 4, and each of 160 probe worlds of one site (40 each of 1, 2, 4 and 12 stands) takes 2 to 4 (leg 1, DEC-1004). R₀ is computed once, from the base radii; a site widened later does not change it. Every b_s is an integer (5.4.1), so the isqrt takes an integer, and R₀ and every later R are integer millimetres (5.4.18). R₀ is a start, not a claim: the outer iteration (5.4.12) widens R when the regions, the sites or the trail do not fit, and only the fixtures state a world's size (5.4.15).

**5.4.5 Regions (the partition).** Kit world: one region, the whole world disc, unnamed. Hero world: each region row's anchor (u, v), integers in thousandths with u² + v² ≤ 10⁶ (validated by 4.16.2), is scaled exactly to (0.8 R u ⁄ 1,000, 0.8 R v ⁄ 1,000), so every anchor lies inside the world disc at a distance of at most 0.8 R from the origin; the disc is partitioned by the **power diagram** of the anchors with weights w_i = (weight_i ⁄ 1,000) × (0.25 R)²: the cell of region i is the set of points x with |x − a_i|² − w_i ≤ |x − a_j|² − w_j for every j, clipped to the world polygon. Each cell is computed by half-plane intersection in region-table order in exact rational arithmetic (5.4.18); the cells are convex polygons that tile the disc exactly, and each is rounded once at emission. Because the anchors and the weights both scale with R, the partition's shape is the same at every R, so the degeneracy check gives the same result on every pass (the reference makes it on each): a region whose cell is **degenerate** (empty, or of zero area, a point or a segment, decided exactly on the rational polygon) is a data error that no widening cures, and the build fails naming the region ("degenerate cell; move the anchor or change the weight"). A cell of positive area grows with R². Each region's **usable cell** is its cell offset inward by the 10 m margin, tested as a clearance (5.4.18); the margin belongs to the trail. The region's row names its kit or variant and its water; the kit dresses the cell (5.6.3), and the pipeline emits the cell polygon so the engine never recomputes the partition.

**5.4.6 Site placement in a region.** Sites are placed in site order, each in its region (an era site in its era's region; a regional site in its region; on a kit world every site in the one region). Before a site is placed, its **local geometry** is computed in the site's own frame from r_s and its PRNG alone: the blob (5.4.7) and the path with its pad, approach and stone (5.4.8); nothing in it depends on where the site lands. Candidate centres are the points of a square lattice of pitch 10 m anchored at the origin that lie inside the usable cell. A candidate is **admissible** if the site's bounding disc (radius b_s) lies inside the usable cell and at least 10 m from the plaza's edge path, from every hero volume and from every already placed site's bounding disc. Among admissible candidates the allocator takes the one **nearest the previous site's centre** in site order (for the first site, nearest the plaza's edge path; for a regional site, nearest the centre of the last era site placed), ties by lower lattice row then column, so consecutive sites are neighbours and the trail's legs are short. If no candidate is admissible the region is **short**, and the outer iteration widens R (5.4.12); a region still short on the twentieth pass fails the build naming the region and the site. Because every cell of positive area grows with R² while a site's bounding disc does not change with R, a region with such a cell and at least one site becomes admissible for a large enough R; the pass bound, not the geometry, is the stopping rule, and a world that needs more than twenty passes fails naming the region and the site. Because every bounding disc lies 10 m clear of every hero volume, of the plaza's edge path and of every other site, and 10 m inside its cell, no hero volume, no part of the plaza and no other site ever touches a blob, a path, a pad, a stone or an approach: hero volumes move sites, they do not clip them; and every site's bounding disc lies at least 10 m from everything outside the site, so the **attachment square** of its approach point (5.4.8), which reaches at most 3.6 m beyond the bounding disc, lies at least 6.4 m from everything outside the site: only the site's own blob can block that square, and the approach test excludes it (5.4.8, 5.4.11).

**5.4.7 Site blob.** The site's blob is a star-shaped polygon about its centre c_s: r(θ) = r_s × (1 + 0.2 × (a₂ cos(2θ + φ₂) + a₃ cos(3θ + φ₃) + a₄ cos(4θ + φ₄)) ⁄ (a₂ + a₃ + a₄)), with a_m drawn as integers in [200, 1000] thousandths and φ_m as integers in [0, 360,000,000) micro-degrees by the site PRNG in the order a₂, φ₂, a₃, φ₃, a₄, φ₄, sampled at 72 equally spaced θ (every 5°) as exact rational vertices (5.4.18), so 0.8 r_s ≤ r(θ) ≤ 1.2 r_s. Nothing clips the blob (5.4.6). Stands may stand only inside the blob.

**5.4.8 Site path (the sweeps), pad, approach and stone.** The site's path is a serpentine of **sweeps**, built in the site's frame. The sweep direction θ_s is drawn from [0, 180,000,000) micro-degrees by the site PRNG (one draw) and **tried**: the sweeps, pad, approach and stone are built for θ_s; if the path is empty, or the approach test or the stone test below fails, θ_s is advanced by 10° and everything in this subsection is rebuilt, up to 18 tries in all (no further draw is taken; the site's stream continues after the one draw whatever the try); if every try fails, the site is **widened** as in 5.4.9. d_s is the unit direction of θ_s and n_s its left normal. Let z(v) = ⟨v − c_s, n_s⟩ over the blob's vertices, z_min and z_max its extremes and w = z_max − z_min the blob's extent across the sweeps. The number of sweeps is m = max(1, ⌊w ⁄ h⌋), at offsets c_k = z_min + (w − (m − 1) h) ⁄ 2 + k h for k = 0 … m − 1, so the family is centred on the **extent**, not on c_s, and every sweep line lies at least h ⁄ 2 inside the extent. Sweep k's **chord** is the longest segment of the line {x : z(x) = c_k} inside the blob (ties by the lower ⟨x, d_s⟩ of its start); sweeps are traversed alternately, sweep 0 in the direction d_s from the end with the lower ⟨x, d_s⟩, sweep 1 against it, and so on. Each chord is **shortened** at both ends. With m the sweep count before any drop: the **head end** of sweep 0 (where travel enters the site) by 1.5 m; the head end of every sweep k ≥ 1 and the far end of every sweep k < m − 1 by 15 m, which is 4.5 m plus the half-turn's radius h ⁄ 2, because a half-turn joins each of those ends; and the far end of sweep m − 1 by 4.5 m. A site of one sweep is shortened by 1.5 m at its head end and by 4.5 m at its far end. The chord of sweep 0 after shortening is the path's first run; its head is the path's **head** H. **Joins.** Sweeps k and k + 1 are joined at the side where sweep k ends: let e_k be sweep k's far end and e'\_{k+1} sweep k + 1's head end, and let t = min(⟨e_k, d_s⟩, ⟨e'\_{k+1}, d_s⟩) when sweep k runs along d_s (max when it runs against it); both chords are **trimmed to t** on that side (trimming only shortens a chord and keeps it inside the blob), and the join is the half-turn: a 180° arc of radius h ⁄ 2 centred at the midpoint of the two trimmed ends, sampled every 15°, bulging outward beyond t by h ⁄ 2. The order is: every chord is shortened, then every join trims its two chords, then the first chord shorter than s is dropped with every sweep after it, and the path ends at the previous sweep's far end. A sweep that a drop leaves last keeps its shortened and trimmed far end. If sweep 0 is dropped the path is **empty** and the try fails. **Wobble.** Each sweep's run is sampled every 0.5 m of its length and at its end point, and each sample is displaced along n_s by A(u) × sin(360° × u ⁄ λ_k + ψ_k), where u is the distance from the run's head end, λ_k is drawn as integer millimetres in [24,000, 40,000] and ψ_k in [0, 360,000,000) micro-degrees (two draws per sweep, in sweep order), and A(u) is 3 m, tapered linearly to 0 over the 6 m nearest each end of the run, so the run's ends are preserved and the half-turns connect exactly. Half-turns are not wobbled. **Head ray, pad and approach.** Let u_H be the unit direction from c_s to H, the site's **head ray**; because the blob is star-shaped about c_s, the ray leaves the blob at H and never re-enters it. The pad is a disc of radius 1.5 m centred at H + 1.5 m × u_H, so the head lies on the pad's edge, the path begins there, and the pad's outer half lies outside the blob. The **approach** is the straight strip of width 3 m from the pad's centre to the **approach point** P = H + 11.5 m × u_H. **Approach test:** the segment from the pad's far edge (H + 3 m × u_H) to P intersects the blob nowhere; and the **attachment square**, the cell of the 5 m routing grid of 5.4.11 that holds P (a point on a cell edge belongs to the lower row, then the lower column), lies at least 5 m from the blob as a filled shape (the filled-shape distance of 5.4.18 (10), so every point of the square, its far corner included, is clear of the blob's 5 m expansion), so that the cell is never blocked by this site's own expanded blob (5.4.11). The square is computed in the site's frame and is the same cell in the world, because site centres lie on the 10 m lattice of 5.4.6 and the grid is anchored at the origin: a translation by a multiple of 10 m maps grid cells to grid cells, so a site that passes the test locally passes it wherever it lands. (Leg 1 found that the second clause is the one that fails: the first clause failed in none of 4,200 probes and in no fixture, and 6 of Dominaria's 26 sites need a second try or a later one, against 5 before the redraw; DEC-1004.) **Stone.** The site's stone (5.4.16) is a disc of radius 0.5 m centred at H + 4 m × u_H + 3 m × n_H, where n_H is the left normal of u_H, or at the mirror point on the right; the side taken is the one whose disc lies farther from the path's first run, ties left. **Stone test:** the stone's disc lies outside the blob and at least 0.5 m from the blob's boundary, from the pad, from the approach strip and from the path's strip, so it stands beside the approach within reach of the pad (its centre is 3.9 m from the pad's centre). **Arc length** t along the path is the polyline length from the pad's edge through every run and half-turn (5.4.18). **Containment.** Every point of the path's centreline lies within 1.2 r_s + 6 m of c_s. A run lies inside the blob and is displaced at most 3 m. A half-turn lies inside the rectangle whose corners are its two trimmed chord ends and the points h ⁄ 2 beyond them. Those four points lie on the chords as first built, and so inside the blob, because a joined end is shortened by 15 m, more than h ⁄ 2. The disc of radius 1.2 r_s about c_s holds the blob and, because a disc is convex, the rectangle too, so the half-turn lies within 1.2 r_s of c_s before rounding. The pad, the approach and the stone lie within 1.2 r_s + 11.5 m of c_s; with the path's half width and the stone's radius, everything the site owns lies within b_s of c_s, and the checker asserts it on the emitted geometry. The attachment square is not the site's: a point of it lies at most 7.08 m from P (the diagonal of a 5 m cell), so the square lies within 1.2 r_s + 18.6 m of c_s, at most 3.6 m beyond the bounding disc (5.4.6, 5.4.11).

**5.4.9 Clusters, candidates and capacity.** Cluster **slots** lie on the path's centreline at arc lengths s, 2s, 3s, … up to the path's length, sides alternating, the first on the left of the direction of travel from the pad; a slot that falls on a half-turn takes the turn's **inner** side (its cluster disc, centred h ⁄ 2 − d = 4.5 m from the turn's centre with radius ρ = 4.5 m, reaches exactly the path's inner edge at h ⁄ 2 − 1.5 m = 9 m), and the alternation continues as if the slot had taken its turn. For slot i the **cluster centre** is the point at distance d from the centreline along the normal on the slot's side, displaced by the site PRNG by an integer in [−1,500, 1,500] mm along the path's direction and an integer in [−1,000, 1,000] mm across it, followed by the slot's cap draw k_i from {3, …, 8} (three draws per slot, in slot order, void or not, so the draw sequence is fixed; a slot after a cut is no slot and takes no draw). A centre whose disc of radius ρ does not lie inside the blob makes the slot **void**; the alternation continues. For a non-void slot, its **candidates** are the points of the global hexagonal lattice (5.4.18, so stands in neighbouring clusters and sites keep the pitch between them) at most ρ − j from the centre that pass five tests, four of them, (a) to (d), on geometry **inflated by j on every side**, and the fifth, (e), on arc length along the path: (a) the point's disc of radius j lies inside the blob; (b) the footprint centred on it, oriented so the image surface faces the candidate's **path vertex**, the nearest vertex of the path's centreline polyline among the vertices whose arc length lies within 6 m of the slot's arc length, ahead or behind (the slot's **window**; ties by the lower vertex index), lies outside this site's path, pad and approach (capacity tests nothing outside the site: the plaza with its edge path, the hero volumes and every other site lie at least 10 m outside the site's bounding disc, 5.4.6, so at least 25 m from its blob, 5.4.1; and the plaza approach and the trail are reserved only at step (6) of 5.4.12, after capacity; placement tests the plaza, the plaza approach, the hero volumes, the trail and the site's stones, 5.4.10); (c) its apron lies outside the same; (d) its corridor, the straight 1.5 m strip from the apron's front edge to the path vertex, lies inside the blob; (e) **the arc length of its path vertex lies within 6 m of the slot's arc length**, ahead or behind, so a corridor never runs to a vertex that is near in the plane but far along the path (on the inner side of a half-turn the nearest vertex of the whole polyline can be the turn's exit, about 33 m ahead along the rounded half-turn). Test (e) holds by the definition of the path vertex in (b) and rejects no point. A lattice point that lies on its path vertex is not a candidate. Capacity and the candidate order read this path vertex, the nearest vertex of the window; placement may face another vertex of the window (5.4.10). Candidates are enumerated in a fixed order: by the index of their path vertex, then by distance to that vertex, then by lattice (row, column). A slot with fewer than 3 candidates is **void** too, except that on a site with fewer than 3 stands the first slot whose candidate count is at least the site's stand count is kept. **Two consecutive void slots cut the path:** it ends at the second void slot's arc length, every later slot is dropped, and the cut shortens the site's path, never its blob; so no two kept slots are more than 2 s = 18 m apart and the first kept slot lies at most 18 m from the pad. The gaps between occupied clusters and the path's tail after the last of them are bounded at placement, where the path is trimmed (5.4.10); that is how 9.3's twenty-metre criterion holds. A cluster's **capacity** is min(k_i, its candidate count); the site's capacity is the sum over its clusters, less one per chapter stone the site needs (5.4.10). If the capacity is below the site's stand count, the site is **widened**: its widening count is incremented, its redraw count returns to 0 (5.4.10), r_s is multiplied by 1.1, its PRNG is reset to its seed, and its local geometry (5.4.7, 5.4.8) and this subsection are recomputed; its larger bounding disc is re-checked at its centre against 5.4.6, against every other site of the pass and not only the sites placed before it, and if it no longer fits there the pass restarts at step (4) of 5.4.12 with the widened r_s kept, so the site is placed again. After 20 widenings of one site without reaching capacity, or without a path and an approach (5.4.8), the build fails naming the site. Capacity counts candidates independently; conflicts between chosen stands are resolved at placement (5.4.10) and the chosen set is validated afterwards, not assumed. So capacity counts more than placement accepts, and a site with capacity can still be redrawn or widened at placement: on Dominaria, 58 of the 89 site widenings come at capacity and the other 31 at placement, beside 42 redraws (5.4.15).

**5.4.10 Placement.** Placement runs after every site of the world has capacity and the trail is reserved (5.4.12). Sites are placed in site order; within a site, clusters in slot order from the pad; within a cluster, candidates in their fixed order. For each candidate the site PRNG draws its **jitter** (an angle in [0, 360,000,000) micro-degrees and a radius in [0, 248] mm, two draws per candidate in candidate order, accepted or not); the candidate is displaced by it. A candidate whose lattice point already holds an accepted stand or a chapter stone of the site is rejected, after its two draws. Otherwise the candidate is **tried at the vertices of its slot's window** (5.4.9), in order of distance from its lattice point, ties by the lower vertex index, so its first try is at the nearest vertex of 5.4.9; the tries take no draw. At each vertex tried, its **yaw** is the direction from the displaced position to that vertex (5.4.18); the footprint and apron are oriented by that yaw and the corridor runs from the apron's front edge to the vertex; and the three are tested **directly on the displaced, oriented geometry**, exact and with the 2 mm margin (5.4.18), against: this site's path, pad and approach, the plaza with its edge path, the plaza approach, the hero volumes and the trail (5.4.11; the exclusions of invariant 9 by geometry type); the footprint and apron of every stand of this site accepted so far; the corridor of every stand of this site accepted so far (a footprint or apron may not lie in a corridor; two corridors may cross only at an angle of 30° or more); every stone of this site, including the chapter stones placed so far; and the containment rules (the footprint inside the blob and inside its cluster's disc); at a vertex other than the nearest, the corridor must also lie inside the blob (test (d) of 5.4.9, exact, with the 2 mm margin). Other sites are not tested: each lies at least 10 m outside this site's bounding disc (5.4.6), and the independent checker asserts every pair (3.2). The candidate faces the first vertex at which it passes every test. That vertex is its **path vertex**: the yaw, the corridor, the emitted vertex index and the trim read it. A candidate that passes at no vertex is rejected, and the report counts it under the cause of its first try. A candidate that passes is **accepted** at its displaced position with that yaw; because the test is on the final geometry with the margin, acceptance is itself the proof of invariant 9 on the emitted integers, and the independent checker (3.2) repeats it. Acceptance in a cluster stops at its capacity and in the site at its stand count. **Withdrawn clusters.** A cluster that would close with fewer than 3 stands is **withdrawn**, unless the site's stand count is reached in it or the site has fewer than 3 stands; a chapter stone does not count as a stand. A cluster that accepts no stand is withdrawn on any site. A withdrawn cluster's stands and its chapter stone are removed and block nothing later, and its lattice points are free again; the chapter state returns to what it was before the cluster; the draws that its candidates took stay taken; and its slot is void. A slot void at capacity (5.4.9) and a withdrawn slot count together: two in a row cut the path at the second, and every later slot is dropped. The site is **exhausted** when its path is cut, or its kept slots run out, while cards remain. **Redraw.** Each site keeps a **redraw count** a: 0 at the start, kept across restarts and across passes over R. When placement exhausts a site at a = 0, a becomes 1 and r_s stays; the site's seed string gains `|1` (5.4.1), so its blob, its sweep-direction tries, its slots and its jitter are drawn again, and the pass **restarts** at step (4) of 5.4.12. When placement exhausts a site at a = 1, the site is widened as in 5.4.9 and the pass restarts at step (4) with the widened r_s kept. A widening for any cause (5.4.8, 5.4.9, or here) returns a to 0. The world PRNG does not change. **Order.** The site's cards, in their 5.4.3 order, are assigned to the accepted positions in acceptance order: clusters in slot order, candidates within a cluster in acceptance order. So an era's chapters run along the path from the pad outward **by cluster**: a stand in a lower slot holds an earlier or the same chapter as any stand in a higher slot, within a cluster the acceptance order holds the chapter order, and the visitor meets the clusters in slot order because the slots lie at increasing arc length (invariant 11). The same data always yields the same picture. **Chapter stones.** In an era site, each non-empty chapter after the first has a chapter stone: it is placed as the first accepted candidate of the chapter, a disc of radius 0.5 m on the candidate's lattice point without jitter, tested against the same exclusions as a footprint; it takes one unit of its cluster's capacity, and the chapter's first stand is the next accepted candidate. Two chapter stones can never coincide, and a stand never stands on a stone's lattice point, because a lattice point holds at most one stand or stone (above). **Trim.** When a site's placement is complete, its path is **trimmed**: let t_end be the larger of the arc length of the last occupied slot plus ρ and the arc length of the farthest path vertex that any accepted stand's corridor ends on, **capped at the path's total arc length** (a path of 20 m whose last occupied slot lies at 18 m has no vertex at 22.5 m; every corridor vertex is a path vertex, so only the slot term can exceed the length); the path ends at the first vertex at or past t_end, which exists because the path's last vertex lies at its total arc length, that vertex is kept, every later vertex is dropped, and the path's reservation shrinks with it. Vertex indices below the end are unchanged, so every corridor's path vertex stays valid; the pad, the approach, the site stone, the chapter stones and the accepted stands are untouched; a trim only removes geometry, so it creates no intersection and breaks no connection, and the checker runs on the trimmed geometry. The path of a site with no stand (5.4.2) has no occupied slot and ends at its second vertex. **Gap bounds.** On a trimmed site path the arc length from the pad to the first occupied slot is at most 18 m (the first slot lies at s and two void slots cut the path, 5.4.9); between consecutive occupied slots at most 18 m (a cluster under 3 is withdrawn and its slot is void, unless an exception of the withdrawal rule keeps it, and void slots never come two in a row); and from the last occupied slot to the path's end at most 10 m (a corridor's vertex lies at most 6 m past its slot, by the window of the path vertex (5.4.9), so t_end is at most the last occupied slot plus 6 m, and the end vertex lies at most 2.75 m past t_end, the vertex spacing on a half-turn; 8.75 m in all). The checker asserts the three bounds on the emitted geometry, and that the path of a site with no stand has two vertices; the layout report lists the largest of each bound per site. After all sites of a world are placed, the independent checker (3.2) runs on the final geometry; a checker failure is a build failure naming the first violating pair and is treated as a defect in the allocator, never as a data condition and never silently retried.

**5.4.11 Reservations and the trail.** Reservations are shapes in exact rational coordinates (5.4.18), made in this order, each checked against the earlier ones under invariant 9: the plaza (a disc of radius 12 m at the origin) and its edge path (the annulus from 12 m to 15 m), holding the arrival point (the world's origin, facing north) and the return portal (8 m south of the centre, facing it) with its pad (the disc centred 1.5 m north of the portal, at (0, −6,500) mm); the hero volumes from the owner's file (used as given: no step clips a volume to the world polygon; a volume that covers the arrival point or the return pad fails the build naming the volume); then, for every site in site order, its path (a 3 m wide strip along the centreline polyline of 5.4.8, with the half-turns), its pad and its approach. Then the **trail** is routed and reserved, and only then the candidates (5.4.9, 5.4.10); the site stones are part of each site's local geometry and are already reserved. **Legs.** The trail is a sequence of legs: from the plaza's approach to the first site's approach point; from each site's approach point to the next site's approach point in site order over the era sites (hero world) or the set sites (kit world); and a **spur** from the trail so far to each regional site's approach point, in site order. **Grid and obstacles.** Routing is on the square grid of pitch 5 m anchored at the origin; a cell is **blocked** when its square intersects any of: the outside of the world polygon; a hero volume; the plaza with its edge path; any site's blob; each of these **expanded by 5 m**, which exceeds the leg's half width plus its wobble. Other legs are not obstacles: where a leg crosses another leg or a site's approach the two strips meet, as invariant 9 lets a path cross a path, and the crossing is not a junction (4.13). Pads, stones and approaches are not obstacles of the grid. A leg that passes a site can still meet its stone or its pad, because a stone stands up to about 5.5 m outside its blob and the wobble moves a leg up to 3 m; the leg check below rejects such a leg (leg 1 found one in 8,544 probe legs that pass a third site, and none on Dominaria before or after the redraw; DEC-1004). **Ends and attachment cells.** A leg's two **anchor points** are: for the plaza, the far end of the plaza approach; for a site, its approach point; for a spur's start, the vertex of the trail so far (its legs are sampled every 0.5 m) nearest the regional site's approach point. The **attachment cell** at an end is the grid cell that contains the anchor point (a point on a cell edge belongs to the lower row, then the lower column). A site's attachment cell is never blocked. It is the attachment square of 5.4.8, which the approach test requires to lie at least 5 m from the site's own blob, so the blob's expansion does not reach it; and it lies at most 3.6 m beyond the site's bounding disc (5.4.8), while every other obstacle lies at least 10 m from that disc and the world's edge at least 10 m from it (5.4.6), so no other obstacle's 5 m expansion reaches it either. The site tests the square in its own frame and the world sees the same cell (5.4.8). The **plaza approach** is placed so that its cell is free too: its far end lies on the circle of radius 25 m about the origin, at the angle nearest the first site's approach point, advanced clockwise in steps of 10° (at most 36) until the strip of the plaza approach intersects no hero volume and the cell holding its far end is unblocked; if no angle serves, the world is **short**. A far end that lies on a grid line can fall, by the edge rule, in a cell that reaches back to 20 m from the origin, inside the plaza's 5 m expansion; that cell is blocked and the approach turns (leg 1 pins such a case in a unit test). A spur's start cell is never treated as blocked on that spur. Its anchor is a vertex of a reserved leg, and the wobble can move that vertex into a blocked cell beside the free cell of its route, where the spur's strip can come within its half width of a blob or a hero volume; the leg check below rejects such a spur (DEC-1004: no spur of Dominaria starts in a blocked cell before or after the redraw, and one spur in 30 probe worlds did, with no overlap). The grid route runs between the two attachment cells by A* with eight-neighbour moves at integer costs 5 (straight) and 7 (diagonal), the octile distance as the heuristic, no diagonal move past a blocked orthogonal neighbour, ties on f by the lower (row, column) of the cell being expanded, and a predecessor replaced only on a strictly lower g; a cell whose centre is within 10 m of a blob costs double, so the trail skirts sites. **Smoothing** runs once along the route from its start: a vertex is removed whenever the straight segment between its neighbours intersects no blocked cell's square. **Attachment.** Each end of the smoothed route is joined to its anchor point by a straight **attachment segment** from the attachment cell's centre to the anchor point, at most 3.54 m long; it is exempt from the blob expansion and the checker tests its strip against every exclusion of invariant 9 like any other. **Wobble.** The route between the attachment segments is sampled every 0.5 m and at its end point and displaced along the left normal of the segment the sample lies on by A(u) × sin(360° × u ⁄ λ + ψ), λ drawn as integer millimetres in [30,000, 50,000] and ψ in [0, 360,000,000) micro-degrees from the world PRNG (two draws per leg, in leg order), with A(u) = 3 m tapered linearly to 0 over the 10 m nearest each end. The leg, its attachment segments included, is reserved as a 3 m wide strip; the approaches and the plaza approach are reservations of their own. **Leg check.** A leg, its attachment segments included, is checked after its wobble against every hero volume and every site's pad, stone and blob, with the margin of 5.4.18 (4), by the allocator's own predicates. Invariant 9 keeps a path from a hero volume and a stone; the pad and the blob are the leg check's own rule, and the independent checker does not test them. Other legs, the approaches and the site paths are not tested: a path may cross a path (invariant 9). A leg that fails the check has no route. **Failure.** A leg with no route (A* exhausts the grid, the leg check fails, or the plaza approach finds no angle) makes the world **short**, and the outer iteration widens R (5.4.12); a leg still without a route on the twentieth pass fails the build naming its two ends. Water crosses the trail, a site path or an approach only at a ford or bridge prefab (8.4.2); the water buffer and the prop clearance of 5.4.1 apply to every reservation here.

**5.4.12 Order of computation, outer iteration and termination.** For one world: (1) sites and assignment (5.4.2, 5.4.3); (2) R₀ (5.4.4); then, with R: (3) regions (5.4.5; the degeneracy check, whose result is the same on every pass); (4) each site's local geometry at its current r_s (5.4.7, 5.4.8, widening a site whose eighteen tries all fail), then site centres in site order (5.4.6); (5) candidates and capacity for every site (5.4.9), widening a short site in place and repeating (4)'s local geometry and (5) for that site until it has capacity, no longer fits at its centre (restart at (4)), or fails; (6) reservations and the trail (5.4.11); (7) placement, chapter stones and the trim (5.4.10); (8) the checker (3.2). **Restart.** A site that no longer fits at its centre after a widening at step (5), or that placement exhausts at step (7) (its path is cut, or its kept slots run out, while cards remain; it is then redrawn or widened, 5.4.10), sends the pass back to step (4): every site centre, reservation and placement of the pass is discarded; every site's widened r_s, widening count and redraw count are kept; every site's PRNG and the world PRNG are reset to their seeds (a site's seed reads its redraw count, 5.4.1). **Widening R.** If a region is short (5.4.6) or a leg has no route (5.4.11), R becomes ⌈11 R ⁄ 10⌉ mm and a new pass starts at step (3), discarding everything after step (2) and keeping every site's widened r_s, widening count and redraw count. **Bounds.** At most 20 passes over R; at most 20 widenings and 21 redraws per site (one redraw at each of its 21 radii), so at most 41 × (number of sites) restarts in a pass; at most 18 sweep-direction tries per site at each radius and redraw count; A* expands each cell at most once; the sweep count is at most the blob's extent over h; every loop therefore has an explicit bound and the allocator terminates. **Failure modes**, each failing the build with its subject named: a set with first printings and no era, or two eras (5.4.3, Appendix F.2); a keyword naming an unknown region (Appendix F.3); a degenerate region cell (5.4.5); a region that cannot hold its sites after 20 passes (5.4.6); a site that cannot reach capacity, or has no path and approach, after 20 widenings (5.4.8, 5.4.9, 5.4.10); a trail leg with no route after 20 passes (5.4.11); a hero volume covering the arrival point or the return pad (5.4.11); and an independent-checker violation on the final geometry (5.4.10), which the procedure as written cannot produce and which is therefore an allocator defect.

**5.4.16 Site furniture.** The stone at a site's pad is a **reading stone** on an era site (5.13), a **waystone** on a regional site and on a set site of five or more stands, and a **marker stone** on a set site under five stands (name and year, no travel). **5.4.17 The rows risk.** From the air a large site is a comb of sweeps 21 m apart. On the ground, what must make it read as a wood with paths: the wobble, so no run is straight; the clusters, so cards are met in groups and never in a line; the varying caps, so no two groups are alike; the 21 m between sweeps, dressed by the kit so a neighbouring sweep is behind trees, water or rock rather than in sight; and the blob shapes, no two alike. The second slice's first question (9.6) judges it on Dominaria. If it fails, the named alternative is a **branching path**: a tree grown from the pad by the same slot, cluster, capacity, widening and failure rules. It is a design to be written, not a switch: an amendment specifies it to the standard of this section and passes Codex before the one re-slice 9.6 allows (11.17). The map is the only place a whole site is drawn.

**5.4.18 Reproducibility rules.** There is one allocator (8.3.3). These rules make its output a pure function of its inputs on every machine that runs the pipeline, so that the fixtures of 5.4.14 are bytes to compare, not figures to approximate. (1) **Units.** Emitted lengths are integer millimetres and emitted angles integer micro-degrees, in the pack and in the fixtures. (2) **Exact arithmetic.** Inside the allocator and the pipeline's checker every quantity is an integer or an exact rational (Python's unbounded `int` and `fractions.Fraction`); no floating-point number is computed, stored or compared anywhere in either, and a floating-point operation in either is a defect. Table inputs are integers (anchors and weights in thousandths, Appendix F.0). (3) **Rounding.** An exact rational q becomes the integer sign(q) × ⌊|q| + ½⌋ (half away from zero, so −0.5 becomes −1). Rounding is applied once to every emitted coordinate and to nothing else, with one named exception, the polylines of (5), which are rounded when they are built because the rounded polyline is the one emitted and the one every later step reads; the emitted integer is what the checker and the engine see. (4) **Margin.** Rounding moves a vertex by at most 0.71 mm, so the allocator decides every exclusion and containment test on the exact geometry with a 2 mm margin: a required clearance of c is tested as c + 2 mm, and a point required inside a shape must lie 2 mm inside it. The checker tests the emitted integers against the nominal figures of 5.4.1. (5) **Square roots** are `isqrt`, the floor of the integer square root, applied to integers only: r_s = isqrt(K_site × n) (5.4.1), R₀ = isqrt(2 Σ b_s²) + 25 m (5.4.4, every b_s an integer) and the segment lengths below; a distance comparison never takes a root: it compares squared distances as exact rationals. **Polylines.** Every site path (5.4.8), every approach and the plaza approach, and every trail leg with its attachment segments (5.4.11) is built in exact arithmetic and then **rounded to integer millimetres vertex by vertex** as soon as it is built, by the formula of (3); the rounded polyline is the emitted polyline and the geometry every later step reads: slots, cluster centres, path vertices, corridors, reservation strips, the attachment of legs and the trim all read the rounded polyline, and a clearance from it is tested with the same 2 mm margin as everything else. This is the one place where rounding precedes emission, and it is the emission of that polyline. A site's path and its approach are built and rounded in the site's frame and moved to the world by the site's centre, a whole number of millimetres, with no second rounding. At a tie whose local and world values have opposite signs, this can differ by 1 mm from rounding the world value, which is the rule of (3) for a site's stands, blob vertices, site stone and cluster centres. *(Erratum 5, DEC-1096, from the delta re-read of PR #137 on DEC-1091, N3: the code's reading, so no byte changes.)* **Segment length and arc length.** A segment of a rounded polyline has the integer length L = isqrt(Δx² + Δz²) in millimetres, and the arc length to a vertex is the sum of the integer lengths of the segments before it; wherever 5.4 uses an arc length (slots at s, 2s, 3s, …; t_end of the trim; the gap bounds of 5.4.10) it is this integer quantity, and the checker computes it the same way. The point at arc length t lies on the first segment whose cumulative length reaches t, at the exact rational fraction (t − t_start) ⁄ L of the way from its start to its end, so it lies on the segment exactly. **Samples.** A run of a sweep (5.4.8) or a leg (5.4.11) is sampled at every 500 mm of arc length from its start and at its end point, the sample at distance u along a segment being start + (u ⁄ L) × (end − start), exact and on the segment; a sample of a sweep's run is displaced along n_s (5.4.8), and a sample of a leg along the table left normal of its segment's direction (7). (6) **Trigonometry.** sin and cos are read from one committed table, `contract/test-vectors/v4/layout/trig.bin`: for every integer k in [0, 360,000), sin and cos of k thousandths of a degree as int32 scaled by 2³⁰, generated once by a committed script and never regenerated at build; the pipeline asserts the file's SHA-256 before use. The file has no header. Record k is the eight bytes at offset 8 × k: two little-endian signed 32-bit integers, sin then cos. The file holds 360,000 records in 2,880,000 bytes, and its SHA-256 is `fa33d5685b879e0f63d26d684e0e39585ce1b6e291b7f1f3543c82949f7bf777`. *(Erratum 2, 2026-10-13, DEC-1014: the first print did not state the layout; read big-endian, sin 30° comes out as 32, not 536,870,912.)* An angle in micro-degrees is reduced modulo 360,000,000 and truncated toward zero to thousandths of a degree before the lookup, and the table value divided by 2³⁰ is used as an exact rational, so a unit direction is an exact rational vector and nothing is ever normalised. The wobble's phase is 360,000,000 × u ⁄ λ + ψ in micro-degrees as an exact rational, truncated for the lookup; π appears nowhere. (7) **Angles from vectors** (the yaw of 5.4.10, the head ray of 5.4.8, the direction of a segment) are found by binary search over the table, decided by the sign of exact cross products. The search finds the count c such that the vector lies on the direction of c or counter-clockwise of it, and clockwise of the direction of c + 1 (0 after 359,999). The vector's side of the sum of those two directions picks the result: c + 1 if the vector lies counter-clockwise of the sum, c if it lies clockwise, and the lower of the two counts if it lies on it. The result is emitted as that count times 1,000 micro-degrees. *(Erratum 5, DEC-1096, from the delta re-read on DEC-1111, N5: the first print asked for the count whose direction is nearest the vector. The lengths of two neighbouring table directions differ by up to 1.3 × 10⁻⁹, so for a vector less than 10⁻¹⁴ rad from halfway between two counts an exact compare of angles can pick the other count. The rule above is the pipeline's, `Directions.count_of` in `exact.py`.)* **Every unit vector is a table direction:** the unit direction of a vector or a segment, and its left normal, are the table's exact rational sin and cos of the angle found this way; a point at a distance ℓ along such a direction is start + ℓ × direction, exact. The table direction of a segment lies within half a thousandth of a degree of the segment's true direction, a deviation of at most 0.0005° (about 8.73 microradians), so a displacement of about 8.73 mm for each kilometre of segment. The figure is illustrated on the schema example of Appendix F.5 and bounds nothing else: nineteen widenings by ⌈11 R ⁄ 10⌉ from that example's R₀ of 560,368 mm give R = 3,427,185 mm, and the displacement over that diameter is under 60 mm; another world, or a synthetic fixture that puts every card in one era, starts from a larger R₀ and reaches a larger diameter, and no section states a maximum. The deviation never decides a test: every test of 5.4 is made on the geometry so built, never on the real-number geometry it stands for, so the deviation changes what is built and never whether what is built passes; nothing is ever normalised. (8) **The hexagonal lattice** has integer axial coordinates (i, k): x = 3,000 i + 1,500 k, z = 2,599 k millimetres; the nearest-neighbour distance is min(3,000, isqrt(1,500² + 2,599²)) = 3,000 mm; with the jitter radius of 248 mm two stands, which stand on two lattice points (5.4.10), are at least 2,504 mm apart before rounding and more than 2,502 mm after it, so the 2.5 m bound of 5.4.1 holds on the emitted integers. (9) **Random draws.** splitmix64 yields 64 bits per draw; an integer in [lo, hi] is lo + (bits mod (hi − lo + 1)), the small modulo bias accepted; every drawn quantity is an integer in the units of (1). (10) **Geometry.** Every shape is one of two kinds: a polygon with exact rational vertices (the world 72-gon, a cell, a blob, a footprint, an apron, a hero volume's outline, and a cluster disc, a pad or a stone as a 72-gon), or a **strip**, a polyline with a half width (a path, a leg, an approach, a corridor). Intersection, containment and clearance are decided by exact predicates on **filled shapes** (a polygon with its interior; a strip with its width): point-in-polygon by the even-odd rule with the boundary counted inside; segment intersection by the signs of exact cross products; the distance between two filled shapes is **zero when they intersect** and otherwise the least distance between their boundaries, the minimum over the vertex-to-segment distances between the two boundaries. Two polygons **intersect** when an edge of one crosses an edge of the other **or a vertex of one lies inside the other** (so a polygon nested inside another, with no crossing, is at distance zero); a point is at distance zero from a polygon when it lies inside it; a segment is at distance zero from a polygon when it crosses an edge or an endpoint lies inside. A strip's distance to a shape is the polyline's distance to that shape less the half width, at least zero: a strip **intersects** a shape when the polyline's distance to the shape is below the half width (a shape wholly inside the strip is caught, because its vertices lie within the half width of the polyline), and a strip is **clear** of a shape by c when the polyline's distance to it is at least the half width plus c. A strip **lies inside a polygon** when every vertex of its polyline lies inside the polygon and every segment of the polyline lies at least the half width plus the margin from every edge of the polygon: no segment can then cross an edge, and the strip's sides stay inside along the whole segment, a concave polygon and its notches included (a polyline that merely clears the vertices of a notch while its width enters it is rejected). A polygon **lies inside a polygon**, with the boundary counted inside, when every vertex of the inner polygon lies inside the outer one and, once each inner edge is cut at every point where it meets the outer polygon's boundary (an outer vertex on the edge, a crossing point, the two ends of a shared stretch), the midpoint of each piece lies inside the outer polygon. Each piece either runs along the outer boundary, between the two ends of a shared stretch, or meets the boundary at its ends at most and so lies wholly inside or wholly outside the outer polygon; in both cases its midpoint tells which, the boundary counted inside. An edge that meets the outer boundary nowhere lies inside, because its ends do, and an edge of zero length is skipped: its point is a vertex, tested already. The inner boundary then lies in the filled outer polygon, and so does the inner interior when the outer polygon is simple, as a blob, a 72-gon and the plaza are. The cuts are exact fractions, compared by cross-multiplication. *(Erratum 5, DEC-1096, from the review of PR #137 on DEC-1091, B6: the first print stated the strip rule only, and the checker's polygon rule passed an outer boundary that enters the inner polygon at a vertex on an inner edge. The delta re-read of that review (N1, N2) added the piece along a shared stretch and the edge of zero length.)* A strip contains nothing. Every distance is compared squared, as an exact rational. No offset polygon, join, cap or corner normal is ever constructed. The predicate fixtures of 5.4.14 (the notch; the nested squares; the notch vertex) are negative tests of the strip rule, the intersection rule and the polygon rule in both checkers. (11) **Curves** are polylines: the blob at 72 vertices, half-turns at every 15° (13 vertices), runs and legs sampled at every 500 mm of arc length from the start and at the end point. (12) **Reference and change control.** The pipeline's allocator and checker are the reference. An operation this subsection does not name (an iteration order, a tie rule, a search) is defined by the reference's code and pinned by the fixtures; a change to any of them changes fixture bytes, and the pull request that makes it says so and re-emits the fixtures under review (8.8.1). The C# checker (8.8.2) re-implements only the predicates of (10) on the emitted integers, with `System.Numerics.BigInteger` wherever a product of products would exceed int64, and never a position. (13) **Code-defined constants and approximations.** Where 5.4 writes a formula with a real-number symbol (√, π, sin, cos, a unit vector, a disc), the code computes the integer or table quantity this subsection names, and the reference and its fixtures pin it: the site-radius constant K_site = 11,459,156 mm² in place of 36 m² ⁄ π (5.4.1); the integer widening steps ⌊11 r_s ⁄ 10⌋ and ⌈11 R ⁄ 10⌉ (5.4.1, 5.4.12); the table directions of (6) and (7) in place of every unit vector, normal and angle; the integer segment lengths, arc lengths and sample points of (5); and the 72-gon in place of every disc (its vertices at the table directions of every 5° times the radius, so a disc of radius r is the polygon of those 72 exact points, and a clearance from a disc is a clearance from that polygon). The one exception is the containment test of 5.4.8 and invariant 9, which reads the circle of radius b_s itself; the 72-gon's vertices lie on that circle up to the table's rounding (at most 5 × 10⁻¹⁰ of b_s), so the two differ by at most 0.1 percent of b_s. Each of these is a definition, not an approximation the code may vary: a change to any of them changes fixture bytes and goes through (12).

### 5.5 Stands

1. A stand is an image surface about 0.63 × 0.88 m (card aspect about 5:7; the surface takes the image's pixel aspect, 4.15.5) on a plinth about 1.1 m tall, facing its apron. It shows the first-printing `normal` image when the avatar is within 25 m, and the swatch colour beyond that. Rarity sets the plinth's material in four steps (stone, bronze, silver, gold-veined); the class sets a thin rim of the class's colour on the plinth's edge, in every kit and at every time of day, gold for multicolour and grey for colourless: this is the **colour cue**, the place colour lives now that it is not geography (Amendment 4; the first slice shipped without the rim, DEC-976). Rarity and class are metadata made visible, not tiers to earn.
2. The image surface is rendered on the isolated path of 8.4.4: unlit, exposure-independent, fog-free, excluded from post-processing, depth-tested against the world. Nothing about the image changes with the scene's lighting or weather. The plinth is lit normally and casts shadows; the image surface receives none.
3. Within 3 m the stand's hover label shows card name, set, year and artist. Interacting (6.1.3) enters card view.
4. Minimum stand spacing is 2.5 m (5.4.1). Each stand has a 1.5 m apron, the point a travel portal lands on, and a corridor to its site's path (5.4.9). Stands stand in clusters of one to eight, three to eight by design (5.4.9, 5.4.10), never in a row.
5. Image loading is nearest-first within the 25 m radius, bounded by the slot count of 8.4.3; the loader never requests more images than there are slots, so residency is a hard bound and 25 m is a radius within which images are preferred, not a promise. A stand whose image has not arrived or has no slot shows its swatch, never a placeholder rectangle. Images fade in over 200 ms.
6. Filtered-out stands (6.5) show their swatch, not a dimmed image, lose their rim glow and are not interactive. A filtered-out stand reached through search is un-filtered on arrival (6.4.6).

### 5.6 Environments: kits, theme rows, hero set-pieces

1. **Twelve kits** cover the roster: `temperate` (meadow, rivers, lakes), `forest`, `coast` (sea, islands, cliffs), `desert`, `volcanic` (lava, ash, basalt), `mountain` (steppe, snow line), `swamp`, `moor` (gothic, fog), `stone-city`, `filigree-city`, `phyrexian` (metal, oil), `void` (the hub, the Blind Eternities' world and the pockets). A kit is a terrain layer set, a water configuration, a vegetation or structure prop set, a sky and lighting rig, a weather set, particle effects and a sound set (5.12). The engineers own the kit code and prefabs; the kit assets are supplied as 8.12 says. Given a theme row, the layout from the pack and a seed, a kit dresses any world size.
2. **Theme rows** (Appendix C) tune a kit per plane: sky, light, palette, weather, water, ambient family, music mood. Two planes on the same kit with different rows read as siblings; two on different kits read as different worlds. A kit world whose row inherits every field from the kit reads as the kit's default; 11.8 schedules the curation.
3. **Region dressing.** On a hero world each region is dressed by the kit and variant its row names (Appendix F.1): ground layers, prop set and density, water, with a 20 m blend across the region boundary and a ford or bridge where water crosses the trail or a site path. A hero world names at most three distinct kits across its regions (11.18); one terrain carries them as per-region layer weights (8.4.5). On a kit world the kit varies its ground layer mix and prop density per site from the site's PRNG, so no two sites look the same. No dressing follows colour class: colour is the cue on the stand (5.5.1). The first print's per-plane wedge dressing (Alara's shards, Lorwyn's two aspects) is now the region table's job; 11.9 is resolved. *(Amendment 4.)*
4. **Hero set-pieces and lore tables.** The twenty entries in Appendix C.1 (nineteen planes and the Blind Eternities) carry two things. First, their lore tables (Appendix F), from which the pipeline lays the world out; by the owner's decision all twenty table sets are accepted before the first distribution (4.16.5, 8.12.7). Second, hand-built geometry by the owner in the editor: a landmark in the plaza and up to one landmark per region, placed by the owner inside a hero volume the owner exports (4.12.2) so the pipeline can keep stands, paths and the trail out; the region row's `set_piece` field is the proposal. Set-pieces are additive and staged: a hero entry whose set-piece scene is missing ships on its kit's dressing of its regions. The first release carries the hub's portal ring (core content) and Dominaria's set-piece; the other nineteen entries' set-pieces follow release by release (8.12.4). *(Amendment 4.)*
5. **Kit worlds.** The 26 planes with cards that are not hero entries ship on their kit and theme row alone, laid out by the journey law's release-order reading (5.4): one region, the sets as sites along the trail, no prose.
6. **Pockets.** The 42 empty planes share the generated `pocket` row: a void space about 20 m across with a single waystone that reads the plane's name and "no cards first printed here", and a return portal.
7. Adding a plane is one Appendix A row, one Appendix B row and one `themes.yaml` entry. The pipeline fails if any is missing.

### 5.7 Sky, light and weather

1. Each world has one sky and one lighting rig from its theme row: time of day, sun or moon elevation and tone, cloud cover, fog colour and density, and optional weather (rain, snow, ash, sandstorm haze, fireflies, embers). HDRP's physically based sky, volumetric fog and volumetric clouds are the implementation; the HDRP low quality level (8.1.1) replaces volumetric clouds with a cloud layer and reduces fog quality.
2. Time of day is fixed per world; there is no day cycle in v4. Clouds and weather move.
3. Weather never obscures a stand within the 3 m interaction radius, and never touches the image surface (5.1.2).

### 5.8 The avatar

1. The visitor moves as a third-person avatar with a following camera (over the shoulder, with an orbit that follows the mouse with no button held, or the right stick, and a zoom range of 2 to 6 m; 6.1.2). *(Amendment 4: the first print said right-drag; the owner asked for no held button on the first slice, DEC-975.)* The avatar has idle, walk, run, turn-in-place, look-at-stand and portal-step animations, and foot IK on terrain.
2. v1 ships **one avatar: Teferi** (Appendix D). There is no avatar selection screen and no roster list in v1; the start screen says "You travel as Teferi." Adding avatars later adds the selection surface, which section 10 risk 1 treats as a game-read risk to be judged then.
3. The avatar is a camera subject. It has no stats, no abilities, no inventory, no health, no stamina bar and nothing to pick up. Walking is 3 m/s, running 6 m/s, with no cost.
4. The avatar's design is an original stylised interpretation of the character, made from the card art as reference, never a copy or projection of card art. The vertical slice uses a placeholder mannequin; Teferi's model is not commissioned before 11.1 is decided.
5. The avatar sets the home plane (Dominaria for Teferi): the visitor arrives in the hub facing that portal, and the travel menu has a "your cards" section (4.14.4).

### 5.9 Portals

1. A portal is a standing ring of about 3 m with a shader surface. Stepping into it plays a 1.5 s transition (surface fills the view, a sound, a cut). A hub portal's world begins loading per 5.3.3; a return portal's hub is always resident.
2. If the destination is not ready when the transition ends, the portal surface **holds** with input locked for up to the 6 s ceiling (7.2); beyond 6 s a loading indicator appears on the held surface. The transition never cuts to an unready world.
3. Every portal is two-way. A world's return portal stands in the plaza (5.4.11).
4. A **travel portal** opens 2 m in front of the avatar, facing it, wherever the avatar stands (a portal has no collision and needs no reserved ground); the avatar walks into it automatically over 1 s, with no steering by the visitor, and arrives on the destination stand's apron facing the stand, or on the destination pad facing the site's stone. The portal closes behind the avatar. For a destination in another world, the travel portal leads straight to that world's destination: the world loads behind the transition with the hold rule, and the hub is not visited.
5. Reduced motion (5.11) replaces the transition with a 0.3 s fade; the hold rule and the automatic walk still apply.

### 5.10 Card view

1. Interacting with a stand enters card view: the camera moves over 0.6 s to frame the card at a fixed on-screen size (about 60 percent of viewport height), slightly off-centre to leave room for the printings panel; the world stays behind the card with a subtle depth-of-field on the world only (never on the card); the avatar steps out of frame. Nothing in the world can occlude the card in card view: the camera is placed inside the stand's apron looking at the image surface, and props keep the 1 m clearance from aprons (5.4.1).
2. The card is the first-printing `large` image on a thin rounded-rectangle solid with the Scryfall card back and a dark edge, as v3 5.6.2, rendered on the isolated path (8.4.4). It tilts toward the pointer with spring damping, ±12°, and settles when the pointer leaves. Cards with the back-image flag (4.13.3) show a flip control.
3. The **printings panel** lists every included printing: set name, year, rarity, artist, ordered by release date, the active printing highlighted. Clicking a row swaps the card face. Above the list: card name, mana cost, type line, oracle text per face, colour identity and rarity as text. Below: "Open on Scryfall" (opens the system browser) and the artist credit for the shown image as text.
4. Esc, the back control or walking input leaves card view and returns the camera to the avatar over 0.6 s. The stand is added to recently visited (6.6.4).
5. Card view pauses nothing in the world: water flows, clouds drift, sound continues.

### 5.11 Motion and reduced motion

1. All motion is delta-time based and must look identical at 30, 60 and 120 fps.
2. With the system reduced-motion setting, or the setting in 6.9: camera transitions drop to 0.3 s fades, the following camera's smoothing is disabled, card tilt is disabled, weather particles stop, vegetation wind stops, water keeps a flat animated surface. Walking is unchanged; the travel menu is the travel aid.

### 5.12 Sound

Three layers plus interface sounds, all original or CC0 licensed with provenance recorded (8.12.3), never Wizards' audio (Fan Content Policy).

1. **Ambient bed.** One loop per ambient family in Appendix C (fourteen families), crossfaded at region boundaries on a hero world (a region row may name its own family, Appendix F.1; the default is the theme row's) so the marsh gains frogs and the coast gains surf; one family over a kit world. Stereo, 1 to 2 MB each as Ogg Vorbis.
2. **Music.** One mood per plane from Appendix C (fifteen moods). v4 ships a small set of original or CC0 ambient loops, one per mood, 2 to 4 minutes each, looped with a crossfade, ducked under card view. Generative music is deferred (11.3).
3. **Spatial prop sound.** Every water body, lava flow, fire, forge, machine, wind-exposed tree line and waterfall emits positional sound with distance attenuation and reverb zones (a cave sounds like a cave). A river is heard before it is seen. Implementation is standard 3D audio sources and reverb zones (8.6); a spatialiser plugin is an upgrade, not a dependency.
4. **Interface sounds.** Portal open, portal step, card view enter, card view leave, waystone use, travel menu open. Six sounds.
5. Master, ambient, music, effects and interface volumes are separate settings. Sound starts on the first input after the start screen.

## 6. Interaction requirements

### 6.1 Controls

1. Keyboard and mouse, and a standard gamepad. Both are complete; nothing requires one or the other.
2. Explore: WASD or left stick to walk, Shift or a trigger to run, mouse or right stick to orbit the camera, scroll or a shoulder button to zoom.
3. Interact (E, a face button, or a click while a stand's label is showing within 3 m; the click has no place of its own, DEC-975) enters card view, or uses a portal, a reading stone or a waystone.
4. Esc or the menu button: leave card view, close an overlay, or return to the hub through the return portal, in that order.
5. M opens the map; T opens the travel menu; `/` opens search; Tab shows the HUD legend; `?` opens help; F12 takes a screenshot.

### 6.2 Modes

1. Four modes: **start** (start screen), **hub**, **explore** (in a world or pocket) and **card view**. Overlays (map, travel menu, search, settings, help) sit over hub and explore.
2. Mode transitions are the portal transition (hub to world and back), the card view transition (0.6 s) and the travel portal. No cut is ever instantaneous except under reduced motion fades.

### 6.3 HUD

1. Top-left: plane name, the region you stand in (hero world) and the site (for example "Dominaria · Shiv · The Brothers' War"; on a kit world "Rabiah · Arabian Nights, 1993"), updated as you cross a boundary; crossing into a region shows the region's sentence beneath it for 4 s (5.13.7). In the hub: "The Hub".
2. Filter chips beneath the plane name, as v3 6.3.2, with a live count of matching stands.
3. The HUD legend (Tab) lists the present colour classes with their rim colours (5.5.1) and the absent ones; it is the key to the stands' rims and the map's dots. Colour is not a direction.
4. **Map (M):** a top-down overlay of the current world: regions as areas named at their anchors (hero world), the trail as a line with the sites' stones numbered in site order, sites as outlines with their chapter stones, the avatar, the return portal, and the stands that match the active filter as dots in their class colour. Clicking a site or a stone opens a travel portal to its pad; clicking a region opens one to the pad of its first site in site order, and a region with no site is named on the map and offers no travel. The map is a travel aid and shows nothing the world does not.
5. The HUD hides in card view except for the printings panel and the breadcrumb, and hides entirely for a screenshot (6.8).

### 6.4 Search

1. Opened by `/` or the search control. A text box over the scene; Esc or clicking outside dismisses it.
2. Matches card names, plane names and set names with fuzzy, prefix-favouring matching; double-faced cards match on either face name; avatar aliases match their cards.
3. Results are grouped Planes, Sets, Cards, up to 8 per group. Arrow keys move, Enter selects.
4. Selecting a plane opens a travel portal to its plaza. Selecting a card opens a travel portal to its stand's apron, in this world or another (5.9.4).
5. Selecting a set reads the set's stand locations from the pack (4.13.4, 4.13.6). ### 7.6 Maintainability

1. Appendices A, B, C and F, `overrides.json`, `avatar-overrides.json` and the hero volumes files are the only curated data inputs; everything else is derived.
2. The pipeline fails loudly on unknown enum values and on any Appendix C violation (4.12.3).
3. Unity, HDRP and every package are pinned in the project manifest; upgrades are deliberate and reviewed.
4. Everything an engineer changes is text: C#, YAML scenes and prefabs, shader graphs serialised as text, ScriptableObjects, assembly definitions, editor scripts. Binary assets (models, textures, audio) are content supplied under 8.12 and committed through Git LFS; engineers do not make them by hand. An engineer on a content task may commit a **derived binary**: a binary that a committed script makes from CC0 source files and from nothing else, such as a model cut to fewer triangles, a clump set from single plants or a texture repacked. Four conditions hold a derived binary to its sources. (a) The kit's `sources.yaml` pins every source by its URL and SHA-256, and names the script and the derived binary's own SHA-256; a source need not be committed. (b) The script is committed, runs from the command line, and names the tool and the version it runs on. (c) The script only cuts, combines, arranges, repacks or renders its sources: it models nothing and paints nothing of its own, it calls no generative model, and it does not tint, darken or lighten a texture. Geometry that the script builds is a cut when the script computes every vertex, UV and normal of it from the sources and gives it no shape of its own. So a script may replace each leaf of a tree with one flat quad that carries the leaf's own rectangle of the texture. It may also replace a group of neighbouring leaves or tufts of needles with one flat quad. That quad lies where the group lies and along the line the group lies along. Where that line is unclear or runs nearly upright, as the trunk does, the quad points out from the trunk. It carries a whole part of the same source's texture, such as a spray that the source's own mesh does not draw. A table in the script that locates such a part must be one that a committed script measures again from the pinned texture. The script may keep a part of the quads and enlarge them, so that the crown covers what it covered. It may turn their normals outward from the trunk or from a point that it computes from the leaves. It may not bend a source into a new shape, such as a rock into an arch, or lengthen a stem into a cane. (d) Run again on the pinned sources, the script makes the committed file again, and review checks that by the comparison of 8.12.2. A binary that an engineer makes by hand, by a script from nothing or with a generative model is not a derived binary and stays out of the repository. *(Amendment 4 erratum 3, 2026-10-13, DEC-1038, the owner's decision on the DEC-1003 card at 09:29Z. The sentences of (c) on geometry that the script builds are two CEO rulings: the leaf's quad after the erratum's review (DEC-1041), and the group's quad after the Founding Engineer's question on a fir's needle cards (DEC-1038). ### 8.4 Scene structure and rendering

1. One persistent scene holds systems (data, audio, UI, avatar, camera rigs, streaming) and the HUD. The hub, each kit world and each hero scene are loaded through Addressables behind the portal transition.
2. A world is: a terrain generated from the kit and the theme row at load (cached per world on disk, 7.4.4), a water configuration placed only in the water the region rows name (Appendix F.1; the theme row's on a kit world) and in the kit's water zones (the world minus the 3 m water buffer around paths, corridors, aprons, pads and stones, 5.4.1; water crosses the trail or a site path only through a ford or bridge prefab), a prop scatter (seeded, instanced, run **after** the pack is read, following each region's kit and variant, and excluding stands, aprons, corridors, the trail, site paths, pads, stones, hero volumes and the 1 m prop clearance around them), the hero scene if any, the stands, waystones and marker stones, the return portal, the sky and lighting rig, reverb zones and audio emitters. The following camera is a Cinemachine rig with a collider that pulls in on props; the 1 m clearance along paths and corridors is what keeps that rare.
3. **Stand images.** Per world one `Texture2DArray` of **384 slots** at 244 × 340 (the `normal` image downscaled by exactly one half, aspect preserved, no padding), RGBA8 with a full mip chain, trilinear sampling, so a stand 25 m away is minified without shimmer. 384 × 332 KB × 1.33 is about 170 MB; with card view's two uncompressed `large` textures (about 5 MB) the resident total is about 175 MB, inside the 192 MB target and the 256 MB ceiling. Decoding and downscaling happen off the main thread; the upload is one `Texture2D` to array-slice copy per image, time-sliced to at most two uploads per frame. No runtime texture compression in v1 (`Texture2D.Compress` yields DXT1 for RGB and DXT5 for RGBA input, is uninterruptible per call, and would change the pixels the colour test compares); it is an optimisation to revisit if GPU memory, not frame time, is the constraint (11.14). Residency: the loader fills slots nearest-first within 25 m and evicts the least-recently-near slot when a nearer stand needs one; the slot count is the hard bound and the 25 m radius is a preference (5.5.5). A world transition releases the old world's array before the new one allocates.
4. **The isolated image path.** Image surfaces (stands in the world and the card in card view) must be opaque to the world's rendering (so fog, exposure, depth-of-field and ambient occlusion treat them as solid objects and nothing renders behind them) and must reach the screen with their exact pixels (no lighting, exposure, fog, post-processing or temporal anti-aliasing). Two renders with matching, unjittered, output-resolution buffers achieve this:
   - **In the main camera**, every image surface is drawn in the opaque pass as an ordinary opaque object with a flat material in the stand's swatch colour. It writes depth and colour like any object, so occlusion, depth-of-field, fog and exposure are all consistent and the world behind it is correctly hidden. Its colour is a placeholder that the composite below replaces.
   - **An exhibit camera**, slaved to the main camera's pose and field of view, renders at output resolution with the **unjittered** projection and with post-processing, anti-aliasing and dynamic resolution disabled. It renders two things into its own targets: an **occluder depth** prepass of the world's opaque geometry within 60 m (terrain, props, hero geometry, avatar, plinths), and then the image surfaces with their exact texels (unlit, exposure-independent, fog-free), depth-tested against that occluder depth, into an RGBA **exhibit buffer** whose alpha is the image surfaces' **complete** coverage: the whole card rectangle as projected, not an inset. The composite must replace every pixel of the main camera's placeholder and lose no pixel of the card; whether the boundary is clean under temporal anti-aliasing, depth-of-field and resolution scaling is not assumed from the independent buffers but **measured** by test (f) below, and row I of the slice gates on it.
   - **Composite.** After the main camera's post-processing and upscaling, a final pass blends the exhibit buffer over the main output where its alpha is set. The exhibit camera's buffers are at output resolution and unjittered, so there is no mismatch with the main camera's jittered or upscaled depth; occlusion is decided by the exhibit camera's own occluder depth; TAA never sees the exhibit pixels; depth-of-field blurs the world (which is in the main camera's colour) and not the card (which is composited after).
   - **Cost and alternative.** The occluder prepass is a second depth-only draw of nearby opaque geometry; the slice measures it. If it costs more than 1.5 ms on the reference machine, the alternative is an HDRP custom pass at the after-post-processing injection point drawing the image surfaces with the unjittered projection against a depth buffer the pass itself re-renders unjittered at output resolution (HDRP documents that the camera's own depth at that point is jittered and may be at render resolution, so it must not be reused); the composite and the opaque placeholder draw stay the same. Row I of the slice's acceptance matrix (9.5) gates both.
   - **Tests** on the pinned HDRP version: (a) colour isolation: a known card rendered at exactly 244 × 340 screen pixels under a bright sky, a dark sky and dense fog, compared to the source image downscaled by the same half-step filter, per-channel tolerance 1 on 255; (b) occlusion: an occluder box placed between camera and stand hides exactly the pixels the main camera hides, to within the 0.5 px inset; (c) motion: twenty frames of camera motion with TAA on produce no frame-to-frame change on a static image surface beyond the tolerance; (d) depth-of-field on in card view leaves the card pixels within tolerance while the world behind it measurably blurs; (e) resolution scale 0.75 keeps (a) within tolerance 2 on 255; (f) boundary: along the entire perimeter of a composited card, with TAA on, with depth-of-field on, and at resolution scales 1.0 and 0.75, no pixel of the swatch placeholder survives and no pixel of the card is lost, to within one output pixel of the exact projected edge, for a static camera and across twenty frames of camera motion. ### F.1 `regions.json`

```json
{
  "plane": "dominaria",
  "regions": [
    {
      "id": "shiv",
      "name": "Shiv",
      "kit": "volcanic",
      "variant": "basalt-steppe",
      "anchor": [700, 200],
      "weight": 1000,
      "water": "lava",
      "ambient": "volcanic",
      "sentence": {"text": "<one sentence on the place, in our words>", "source": "https://magic.wizards.com/en/planes/dominaria"},
      "set_piece": "a proposal for the owner's landmark in this region",
      "sources": ["https://magic.wizards.com/en/planes/dominaria"]
    }
  ]
}
```

The row names places and weights, never positions in metres: the allocator places (5.4.5, 5.4.6). The anchor is a direction and a rough distance from the plaza inside the unit disc; the weight grows or shrinks the region against its neighbours. A region may hold no site: it is then scenery the trail crosses, and the map names it without a travel link (6.3.4).

### F.2 `eras.json`

```json
{
  "plane": "dominaria",
  "kind": "lore",
  "accepted": {"by": "owner", "on": "2026-10-20", "issue": "DEC-9xx", "digest": "sha256:<64 hex>"},
  "eras": [
    {
      "id": "brothers-war",
      "name": "The Brothers' War",
      "years": "<the era's place in the plane's history, in words>",
      "region": "argoth",
      "sets": ["atq", "bro", "brc"],
      "headline": [],
      "paragraphs": [
        [
          {"text": "<sentence>", "source": "https://magic.wizards.com/en/story/..."},
          {"text": "<sentence>", "source": "printing:<scryfall_id>#front"}
        ]
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

**Acceptance and its digest.** `digest` is `sha256:` followed by the SHA-256 of the **canonical form** of the three files concatenated in the order regions, eras, keywords, with the `accepted` member removed from `eras.json` before serialisation. The canonical form of a file is its parsed object re-serialised with keys sorted by Unicode code point, no whitespace, non-ASCII characters written as themselves in UTF-8 with only the escapes JSON requires, and every number an integer in plain decimal (F.0 allows no other number), exactly as the pipeline's one serialiser writes it (`json.dumps(obj, sort_keys=True, separators=(',', ':'), ensure_ascii=False)`); only the pipeline computes the digest, and the engine reads the record as data. Any edit to any value, any re-ordering of an array and any change to a source changes the digest. The owner's acceptance names the digest on the tables' issue; the pipeline recomputes it on every run and derives the **acceptance status**: **accepted** (a record is present and its digest matches), **stale** (present, does not match: the tables changed after acceptance) or **absent**. The status is reported on every run; a development build proceeds in every status; a distribution build fails on stale or absent, naming the entry (4.16.5). The status is distinct from validity: a file that violates F.0 or 4.16.2 fails the build in both modes whatever its record says.

**Product tables (the Blind Eternities).** With `kind: product`, a region is a **set family** (Commander decks, Masters and reprint sets, supplemental and silver-bordered sets, promotional and box-set printings, and so on) carrying its `sets` list, and an era is **one family over one release period**: its `region` is the family's region and its `sets` lie in that region's list, so every card stands in its family's region and the trail runs through the families in the order the table gives. The era ceiling is 24 for a product table. The text describes the products that printed the cards, in our words, from Scryfall's and Wizards' set pages (5.13.8).

### F.3 `keywords.json`

```json
{"plane": "dominaria", "keywords": [{"word": "Shivan", "region": "shiv"}, {"word": "Tolarian", "region": "tolaria"}]}
```

`word` is matched as a whole word on any face name, case-insensitive and Unicode case-folded (5.4.3); table order is precedence; a word maps to exactly one region. For a product table the array must be empty (4.16.2): every card of the Blind Eternities stands in its family's region (F.2) and no keyword can move it. The report lists every match and every card that matches words of two regions. On dataset `a5846bec28d1447e`, 326 of Dominaria's cards carry one of 24 such words (Tolarian, Shivan, Urborg, Benalish, Llanowar, Keldon, Yavimaya, Phyrexian, Otaria, Vesuva, Zhalfir, Jamuraa, Thran, Argoth, Koilos, Serra, Academy, Femeref, Kjeldor, Balduvia, Suq'Ata, Sarpadia, Icatia, Ice Age; counted on Scryfall on 2026-10-12). The drafter chooses the words; the report shows what they move.

### F.4 Sources

A `source` is a **locator** of one of five forms, and nothing else validates:

| Form | What it points at | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| `https://magic.wizards.com/<path>` | a story, a plane page or a set page | the parsed host is exactly `magic.wizards.com`; scheme `https` |
| `https://scryfall.com/<path>` | a card or set page | parsed host exactly `scryfall.com` |
| `https://mtg.wiki/<path>` | an article | parsed host exactly `mtg.wiki` |
| `card:<oracle_id>` | the card's name and type line | the id is in the dataset |
| `printing:<scryfall_id>#front` or `#back` | the printed flavour text of that face of that printing | the printing is in the dataset and the face exists |

A host is validated by parsing the URL, never by a substring match. **What a source is.** A source supports a sentence: the reviewer reads the source and confirms the sentence's facts are there. A source is never copied: the MTG Wiki's text is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (read 2026-10-12) and would bind ours, and Wizards' story text is theirs. The reviewer therefore checks two things per sentence, **support** and **independent wording**, and the verdict records for each sentence the passage relied on (a short quotation inside the review, which is review material, never product text) and that the sentence is not that passage reworded. One source per sentence is bookkeeping that makes the check possible and shows the visitor where to read more; it is not a licence determination, and the product's classification stays the owner's gate (11.1). The visitor sees every source of an era's sentences and of its region's sentence in the reading stone's fold (5.13.1).
