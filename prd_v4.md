## 1. Summary

Eternities v4 is a free desktop application in which you walk the planes of the Magic: The Gathering multiverse as a planeswalker and look at the cards that were first printed there. You start in the hub, a dark space with one portal per plane. You step through a portal and arrive on a world: flat terrain built for that plane, with its own sky, light, weather, water, vegetation or metal or stone, ambient sound and music. The cards of that plane stand on the terrain as flat exhibits, laid out by a law you can learn: a trail runs through the plane's history, each era of that history is a site with a reading stone, and the cards of an era stand in small groups beside the site's winding path. On a hero world the regions you cross are the places the plane's lore names; on a kit world the trail runs through the plane's sets in release order. Walk up to a card and the view changes: the card fills the frame, the world stays behind it, and a panel lists every printing to click through. Leave the view and keep walking. A travel menu opens a portal to any card by name.

It is a place you visit, not a game you play. There are no objectives, no score, no combat, no inventory, no progression and no winning state. Everything else about it, the environments, the third-person avatar, the lighting, the sky, the props, the sound and the feel of moving, is built to the standard of a modern 3D game.

Everything visible is still derived from data. The Python pipeline of v3 keeps every inclusion rule, the first-printing rule and the plane assignment. It gains one curated table for every plane (Appendix C: one theme row per plane) and, for each of the twenty hero entries, three curated lore tables (Appendix F: regions, eras, keywords) written in our own words from named sources, and emits a second data pack for the engine. The renderer moves from the browser to Unity 6 with the High Definition Render Pipeline. The shipped web application stays deployed as the web edition, refreshed with the same data, and remains the screen-reader path to the catalogue.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

### 2.1 Goals

1. **A place, not a game.** Eternities is an explorable gallery. Every feature must pass the test "would this still make sense in a museum?" Walking, looking, reading, travelling and listening pass. Points, quests, timers, rewards, enemies, unlocks and achievements fail. The Fan Content Policy forbids Wizards' IP in games or game components, free or not, and privacy of distribution is no exemption. This document cannot settle whether a walkable 3D gallery with a named avatar is a game in the policy's sense; section 10 risk 1 and open question 11.1 make that an owner decision, with a legal read, before the build is given to anyone outside the project, friends included. Until then every design choice is made to keep the product on the gallery side of the line.
2. **Game-grade feel.** Environments, avatar, lighting, sky, weather, water, props, sound and movement are built to the standard of a current 3D game. A plane with rivers and lakes shows and sounds like water; a plane of lava and ash shows and sounds like fire. This is the product's reason to exist after v3's "mesmerising by default", and it is a goal, not a polish item.
3. **Planes differ, in stages.** At every release a visitor can tell which **kit** they are on without reading a name, and two worlds on the same kit differ by their theme rows (sky, light, palette, weather, water, sound). The twenty largest entries of the roster (92.0 percent of all cards, including the Blind Eternities) are **hero entries**: each is laid out from its own lore tables (regions, eras, keywords; Appendix F), so its geography is the lore's and its trail is its history, and each additionally gets hand-built hero set-pieces that make it unmistakable. By the owner's decision of 2026-10-12, all twenty table sets are written and accepted before the first distribution, and every hero world is built from its tables (4.16.5, 8.12.7). Set-pieces still ship in stages, Dominaria first (8.12.4), and a hero entry without its set-piece is held to the kit standard for its set-piece alone. Pockets are identical by design: they hold nothing but a name.
4. **The picture is the data.** Where a card stands, which site and cluster it belongs to, where the trail runs, how large a world is, which portals exist: all derived from real card data, the curated tables and the fixed seed by one deterministic, terminating allocator (5.4). Nothing decorative is fabricated where a data-driven form is possible. The curated inputs to the picture are Appendix C and, on hero entries, the three lore tables of Appendix F; the tables name places, eras and sources, never positions.
5. **Findable.** Any card, plane or set is reachable by search and by the travel menu without walking. Selecting a destination moves the avatar there through a portal without the visitor steering; the bench times it for same-world and cross-world destinations (7.2).
6. **Maintainable by one person.** Curated inputs are Appendices A, B, C and F, `overrides.json`, `avatar-overrides.json` and the hero volumes files. The pipeline fails loudly on anything it cannot place. Adding a set is one Appendix B row and one run, plus, on a hero entry, its code in an era row of that entry's table (a set with first printings and no era fails the build, 5.4.3); adding a plane is one row in each appendix. Hero set-pieces and avatars are the owner's own work and are additive to a working kit.
7. **Free, private, unofficial, compliant.** No accounts, no telemetry, no server. The Fan Content Policy notice is on the start screen. Scryfall is credited. Card images are shown as complete cards, flat, undistorted, untinted, with the artist line the card itself carries and a text credit beside it.

### 2.2 Non-goals (v4)

1. Any game mechanic: score, quests, objectives, timers, combat, inventory, collection meters, progression, unlocks, achievements, leaderboards, saving "progress" beyond settings and the recently-visited list.
2. Multiplayer or any online component beyond fetching images.
3. A web edition of v4. The v3 web application stays deployed and data-refreshed (8.11). A Unity web export is possible later and is not a goal.
4. Mobile, tablet, touch, VR.
5. Voice acting, licensed Wizards music or video, Wizards logos, set symbols, the planeswalker symbol or mana symbols as authored assets.
6. Steam or any store that frames the product as a game.
7. Universes Beyond and Secret Lair content, *Un*-sets, digital-only sets, tokens, emblems, art series, promos, oversized cards (as v3 2.2.3 and 2.2.4).
8. Gameplay data: prices, format legality, rulings, decklists, collection tracking.
9. Lore content beyond the reading layer of 5.13: no character dialogue, no story text quoted from Wizards, no prose on kit worlds beyond set names and years. *Amendment 4: the first print excluded all lore text; the owner reversed that for the reading stones of hero worlds on 2026-10-12 (5.13, Appendix E.10).*
10. Automatic data refresh; the pipeline runs by hand.
11. Localisation; English only.
12. More than one avatar in v1 (Appendix D); no avatar selection screen in v1.
13. A second render pipeline (URP) in v1 (8.1.1).
14. Basic-land signposts, generative music, runtime texture compression, and screen-reader support in the native build. Each is recorded in section 11 with the reason it waits. *Amendment 4: per-plane wedge dressing left this list; the region tables do that work (5.6.3, 11.9).*

## 3. Domain model

Section 3 of `prd_v3.md` applies: entities (multiverse, plane, Blind Eternities, set, card, printing, face), relationships, invariants and the first-printing consequence. The following entities, invariants and vocabulary are added.

### 3.1 Added entities

| Entity | Definition | Identity |
|---|---|---|
| **World** | The rendered form of a plane with at least one card, the Blind Eternities included: one terrain, one sky, one sound set, the plane's cards standing on it. | plane slug |
| **Hub** | The arrival space: a compact void with the portal ring. It holds no stands. It is not the Blind Eternities' world; the Blind Eternities has a world of its own like any plane. | `hub` |
| **Pocket** | The rendered form of a plane with zero cards: a small void space that names the plane. All pockets share one theme row and are identical apart from the name. | plane slug |
| **Region** | A named place of a hero world from the plane's lore, one row of the region table (Appendix F.1): a kit or variant, a rough position, a weight, its water. The allocator turns the rows into a partition of the terrain (5.4.5). A kit world has one unnamed region: the whole terrain. | world + region id |
| **Era** | A period of a hero plane's history in lore order, one row of the era table (Appendix F.2): its sets, its region, its text, its sources. Every set with first printings on the plane belongs to exactly one era. | world + era id |
| **Keyword** | A word of a hero plane's lore that names a region, one row of the keyword table (Appendix F.3). A card whose name carries the word stands in that region (5.4.3). | world + word |
| **Plaza** | The arrival clearing of a world: a 12 m disc at the origin at the head of the trail, holding the arrival point, the return portal and the hero landmark if any. No stands. | world |
| **Site** | One exhibit area: a blob of terrain with one winding path, its clusters, one pad and one stone. Three kinds: an **era site** (hero world: the cards of one era), a **regional site** (hero world: the keyword cards of one region whose era site lies elsewhere) and a **set site** (kit world: the cards of one set). | world + site key |
| **Cluster** | One to eight stands beside one slot of a site's path, facing it; the law aims at three to eight, and a cluster under three is permitted only as a site's last or on a site of fewer than three cards (5.4.9, 5.4.10). The unit of world streaming (7.3.3). | site + cluster index |
| **Chapter** | The run of stands in an era site that hold one set, in release order along the site's path, marked, after the first chapter, by a chapter stone at the first lattice point of the chapter (5.4.10); a chapter whose cards all stand at regional sites is empty and has no stone. | era site + set code |
| **Trail** | The reserved road through a world: from the plaza to every site's pad in site order, with a spur to every regional site's pad (5.4.11). Water crosses it only at a ford or bridge. | world |
| **Path** | The reserved walkway network: the trail, every site's winding path and the plaza's edge path. | world |
| **Stand** | The exhibit of one card: an image surface on a plinth at a fixed position in a cluster, with a reserved apron in front of it and a corridor from the apron to its site's path. | `oracle_id` |
| **Corridor** | The reserved 1.5 m wide strip from a stand's apron to the nearest point of its site's path. Reserved against props and water, not against walking. | stand |
| **Portal** | A threshold between two spaces: hub to world, world to hub, or a travel portal that opens in front of the avatar and leads to a stand's apron or a site's pad. | source + destination |
| **Pad** | A reserved disc of radius 1.5 m where a traveller arrives: one at every site's path head, one in front of every hub portal, and the return portal's. | world + owner |
| **Stone** | The marker beside a site's pad: a **reading stone** on an era site (the era's text, 5.13), a **waystone** on a regional site, on a set site of five or more stands and beside every hub portal pad (a name, years, a count), a **marker stone** on a set site under five stands (a name and a year). A **chapter stone** marks a chapter inside an era site. Reading stones and waystones offer travel (6.6.2); marker and chapter stones do not. | world + site, or chapter |
| **Lore tables** | The three curated tables of a hero entry (Appendix F): regions, eras, keywords. Their text is the only authored prose in the product. | plane slug |
| **Avatar** | The planeswalker the visitor moves as. v1 ships one, Teferi (Appendix D). | planeswalker subtype |
| **Theme row** | The curated Appendix C row that selects a plane's kit and tunes it. | plane slug |
| **Kit** | A procedural biome: terrain layers, water, vegetation or structure props, sky, lighting rig, weather, particles and sound set. Twelve kits (5.6). | kit name |
| **Hero volume** | A simple shape (box or cylinder) exported from the editor by the owner, inside which the pipeline places no stand, apron, corridor or pad and the kit places no prop. | world + volume id |

### 3.2 Added invariants

6. Every plane in Appendix A is reachable from the hub through exactly one portal, the Blind Eternities included: 88 portals.
7. Every included card has exactly one stand, in the site that 5.4.3 assigns it (its era's site, the regional site of its keyword's region, or its set's site), in the world of its plane.
8. Stand positions, region cells, site centres, blobs, site paths, clusters, the trail, aprons, corridors, pads, stones, world size and portal placement are a pure function of the input data, the appendices, the lore tables, the hero volumes files and the fixed seed, computed by the allocator of 5.4 in integer millimetres. A refresh may move them; a release never changes them under the user. Stands are fixed once the pack is emitted; no runtime system moves a stand.
9. **Exclusions by geometry type, checked on the final (jittered) positions.** No two stands are closer than 2.5 m. A stand's footprint and apron intersect nothing else: not a path, not a pad, not a stone, not a corridor of another stand, not the plaza, not a hero volume, not another stand's footprint or apron. A corridor intersects no footprint or apron of another stand, no stone, no hero volume and no plaza, and ends on its site's path; corridors may cross each other and may cross a pad's edge. A pad lies on the path network by design (a site pad at its path's head, where the trail arrives; a hub portal pad on the hub's ring) or inside the plaza (the return portal's pad), and intersects no footprint, apron, hero volume or other pad. A stone stands beside its pad (its centre 2.5 m from the pad's centre, 5.4.8) or at a lattice point inside a cluster (a chapter stone, 5.4.10) by design and intersects nothing else: no footprint, apron, corridor, path, pad, hero volume or other stone. A hero volume may overlap the plaza but not the arrival point, the return pad, the trail, any site path or any site's bounding disc (5.4.6). Every stand lies inside its site's blob and within the cluster disc of its cluster; every point of a site's path, pad and stone lies within its bounding disc (5.4.8). The independent checker applies exactly these rules to the emitted geometry, lists violations in the report, and the build fails on any.
10. The path network is connected: the trail reaches every site's pad from the plaza's edge path, every site path begins at its pad, every corridor reaches its site's path; and the kit may not block a path or a corridor except with a ford or bridge prefab. The checker asserts connectivity on the emitted network.
11. In an era site, stands run in chapter order along the path from the pad outward, and every chapter after the first has its chapter stone; on a hero world, every stand whose card has a selected keyword (5.4.3) stands in that keyword's region, and every other stand of a hero world stands in its era's region. The checker asserts both on the emitted geometry against the tables.

### 3.3 Vocabulary

One term per concept throughout this document and the codebase. These replace the v3 star vocabulary; v3's data terms (plane, card, printing, first printing) are unchanged.

- **plane** for the domain object, **world** for its rendered form; never "level", "map" (except for the HUD map), "galaxy" or "setting".
- **hub** for the arrival space; never "lobby" or "menu".
- **stand** for a card's exhibit; **image surface** for the part that shows the card; never "pedestal" or "card object". **Panel** is used for exactly one thing: the card view's printings panel (5.10.3).
- **plaza**, **region**, **era**, **keyword**, **site**, **cluster**, **chapter**, **trail**, **path**, **corridor**, **pad**, **stone** (reading stone, waystone, marker stone, chapter stone), **portal**, **apron**, **hero volume**, **lore tables** as defined above. Never **wedge**, **band** or **ring**: Amendment 4 retired them, and the hub's portal **ring** (5.3.2) is the one remaining use of that word.
- **avatar** for the planeswalker the visitor moves as; never "character" or "player model".
- **explore** for the third-person walking mode, **card view** for the focused mode, **map** for the top-down HUD overlay.
- **travel** for any movement that is not walking: portals, waystones, the travel menu.
- **kit** for a procedural biome, **theme row** for a plane's Appendix C row, **hero set-piece** for hand-built geometry on a hero entry.
- **internal evaluation** for use by the owner and the agents; **distribution** for giving the build to anyone else, friends included.

## 4. Data requirements

Section 4 of `prd_v3.md` applies in full: sources, identity, printing inclusion, card exclusion, first printing (as amended 2026-09-04), plane assignment, roster, retained fields, pipeline outputs, refresh, attribution. The following subsections are added.

### 4.12 Appendix C: plane theme table

1. **Source form.** Two YAML files under `pipeline/data/`: `kits.yaml` with one entry per kit (5.6.1) holding every field's default, and `themes.yaml` with one entry per plane in Appendix A that has at least one card, plus one `pocket` template entry. A theme entry names its `kit` and may set any field; a field it does not set is taken from the kit, and the report prints the resolved row so inheritance is explicit, never silent. A plane with cards and no entry fails the build. The 42 empty planes are not listed by hand: the pipeline generates their rows from the `pocket` template each run and lists them in the report.
2. **Fields and allowed values.** `kit` (one of the twelve kits); `hero` (true or false; true only for the twenty entries in Appendix C.1); `sky` in {clear, cumulus, overcast, storm, night, starfield, haze, dusk, dawn}; `light` as `{elevation: degrees, tone: warm | neutral | cold}`; `palette` as exactly three sRGB hex colours; `weather` in {none, fog, rain, snow, ash, sandstorm, fireflies, embers}; `water` in {none, river, lake, river+lake, sea, canals, marsh, lava, oil, waterfall}; `ambient` in {meadow, forest, surf, desert-wind, volcanic, alpine-wind, marsh, moor-wind, city, workshop, machinery, void, jungle, campus}; `music` in {heroic, grand, gothic, serene, epic, mythic, adventurous, whimsical, solemn, bright, industrial, ominous, swing, western, ambient}; `set_piece` (free text, a proposal for the owner, not validated beyond being a string); `hero_volumes` (path to the exported volumes file, hero rows only, may be absent until the owner exports one); `lore` (hero rows only: the directory of the entry's lore tables, `pipeline/data/lore/<slug>/`, Appendix F; required on every hero row; 4.16.5 says when its absence or non-acceptance fails a build); `standard` on a kit entry in `kits.yaml` (`basic` or `photographic`, 8.12.6).
3. **Validation.** Unknown kit, unknown value, a `hero: true` outside C.1, a hero row without a `lore` field, a palette with other than three colours, or a hero volumes file that fails to parse, each fails the build. The lore tables validate under 4.16.2.
4. **Emission.** The resolved rows are emitted into the engine data pack as `themes.json` (4.13). The kit defaults are emitted as `kits.json` so the engine never carries a second copy of the table.

### 4.13 Engine data pack (contract v4)

1. The pipeline emits a second artefact directory for the engine, content-hashed exactly as the web pack (data contract v3 §1), under `unity/Eternities/Assets/StreamingAssets/data/<hash>/`. This is **contract v4**, documented in `docs/data-contract-v4.md`, with byte-level test vectors under `contract/test-vectors/v4/` asserted by both the Python encoder and the C# decoder. The web pack and contract v3 stay frozen for the web edition (8.11).
2. **Files.** `manifest.json`; `planes.json`; `stands.bin`; `sets.bin`; `sets.json`; `search.json`; `themes.json`; `kits.json`; `avatars.json`; `worlds/<slug>.json` (per world, the layout geometry, 4.13.5); `lore/<slug>.json` (per hero entry, 4.16.3); `planes/<slug>.<n>.json` card detail shards (as v3, including the `artist` field per printing and the `story_spotlight` flag, 4.16.4) sharded at 2,000 cards in stand order.
3. **`stands.bin` record, 48 bytes, little-endian, one per included card, ordered by plane index, then site index, then cluster index, then by `oracle_id` bytes.** *(Amendment 4: the band index became the site index, and the cluster index took two of the reserved bytes.)*

| Offset | Size | Type | Field |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 16 | bytes | `oracle_id`, RFC 4122 network order (as contract v3 §6.1) |
| 16 | 1 | uint8 | plane index into `planes.json` |
| 17 | 1 | uint8 | colour identity mask: bit0 W, bit1 U, bit2 B, bit3 R, bit4 G; 0 means colourless |
| 18 | 1 | uint8 | class: 0 W, 1 U, 2 B, 3 R, 4 G, 5 gold, 6 colourless (derived from the mask; stored for the renderer; the decoder asserts agreement) |

The flip control shows only when this is 1 |

The file begins with the 16-byte binary header of contract v3 §2 with version 4. 28,603 records are 1,372,944 bytes before compression. Decoder assertions at launch: class agrees with mask; plane index in range. At the shard load: shard and index resolve to the same `oracle_id`; on a regional site the name carries the keyword. At world entry, against `worlds/<slug>.json`: the site index is within the site count; the set id is in an era or set site's list, or the site is regional; the cluster index is within the site's cluster count; the stand's corridor point in the world file lies on its site's path. The **stand index** is the record's position in this file; `worlds/<slug>.json` and `search.json` refer to stands by it. The record order is not the placement order: 5.4.10 assigns cards to positions in acceptance order, and this file sorts a cluster's records by `oracle_id`; the join is always by stand index or `oracle_id`, never by position within a cluster. *(Amendment 4 draft 2: `x`, `z` and yaw became exact integers and the reserved bytes were used; Codex F52.)* Test vectors include a split card, an adventure card, a transforming card, a meld component, a card with 65,535 or more printings (synthetic) and a malformed join that must be rejected.

4. **`sets.bin`** keeps the v3 structure: per stand, in stand order, the list of included-printing set ids (uint16), so the set facet matches a card by any included printing at every level without loading a shard. **`sets.json`** carries set id, code, name, release date, set type, **effective plane slug** (the plane the set's first printings landed on through Appendix B directly or through a parent set under v3 4.6.3, or null when the set has no first printings), parent set code, and cards first printed there. A set has a site on a plane if and only if it has first printings there; the search and travel surfaces read that from the emitted site list, never from whether the set has its own Appendix B row.
5. **`planes.json`** carries per plane: slug, display name, kind (world, pocket), hero flag, whether its lore tables are accepted, card count, release-ordered set list (id, code, name, year, cards first printed), world radius, terrain extent, theme row key, region count, site count, the arrival pose, the return portal pose and the hero volume list. The hub's portal ring order and poses are carried under `hub`. **`worlds/<slug>.json`**, loaded on world entry, carries the geometry in integer millimetres: regions (id, name, kit, variant, water, cell polygon), the plaza, the trail (polylines with leg ids and junctions), the site list in site order (key, kind, region id, era id or set id, centre, blob polygon, path polyline, pad pose, stone kind and pose, cluster list with centre, slot arc length and stand count, chapter list with set id, first stand index, first cluster index and its travel destination: the chapter stone's pose, or the pad for a first chapter), a per-stand array indexed by stand index (the corridor's point on the site path and the arc length along it, in millimetres), per region its first site index or none, the trail's attachment segments, and the pads.
6. **`search.json`** as v3, plus for each card its plane index and stand index so a result resolves to a stand without a shard load, for each set the list of (plane index, site index, chapter index) triples where it has stands, and for each hero region its (plane index, region id). **`avatars.json`** per 4.14. **`themes.json`** and **`kits.json`** per 4.12.4.
7. The pipeline report gains: stands per world, world radius, outer passes used, region cell areas, sites per world, clusters and path length per site, widenings per site, trail legs and their lengths, minimum spacing achieved, path connectivity result, hero volume intersections resolved, keyword assignments and two-region matches, hero entries without accepted tables (4.16.5), the Appendix C resolved rows, and the invariant-check results of 3.2.

### 4.14 Avatar roster derivation

1. The roster is the set of planeswalker subtypes that appear after the em dash in the type line of any face of any included card whose type line contains "Planeswalker" (`Legendary Planeswalker — Teferi` yields `Teferi`; a transforming planeswalker's back face counts). Multiple subtypes on one face each count. The subtype string is the display name; `avatar-overrides.json` may add aliases (a name the search also matches) and a display name.

Ties between cards with the same release date break by set code then collector number, as v3 4.5.1.

Fixtures (asserted every run): the data-derived home plane of Teferi is the Blind Eternities, because the earliest included Teferi planeswalker card is Teferi, Temporal Archmage (Commander 2014); `avatar-overrides.json` sets Teferi's home plane to Dominaria and the fixture asserts both the raw and the overridden value. ### 4.15 Scryfall on a native client

1. At runtime the application contacts only Scryfall's image CDN, which is two hostnames: `cards.scryfall.io` for card faces and `backs.scryfall.io` for the card back (5.10.2). Every other mention of "Scryfall's image CDN" in this document (7.5.1, 8.10, 9.1.6, Appendix E.8) means these two hostnames, and the loader refuses a redirect to any other host. It never calls the Scryfall API (`api.scryfall.com`); every card fact it shows is in the data pack. The pipeline, as in v3, fetches bulk data at build time. *Amendment 2 (2026-10-10, DEC-952): the first print said "exactly one host"; the card back is served from a second hostname, and a loader built to one shows no card back.*
2. Images are fetched at the size the view needs: `normal` (488 × 680) for stands, `large` (672 × 936) for card view. `small` and `art_crop` are not used. Images are never bundled, mirrored or bulk-downloaded. Scryfall's Imagery page (read 2026-10-10) lists WEBP sizes `grid` and `display` as replacing `normal` and `large` and announces no retirement, so the JPG sizes stay in use; the loader keeps each size's name and file extension in one table, and the refresh runbook's terms re-read includes that page. *(Amendment 2 note.)*
3. An on-disk cache under the OS application-support directory holds fetched images, capped (default 2 GB, least recently used eviction, user-adjustable). Clearing it is a settings action. The cache is filled only by images a view requested (5.5.5, 5.10.2): the hub's world preload (5.3.3) fetches no card image, nothing else prefetches, warms or crawls ahead of the visitor, and the cache is never exported, shared or served to another device. Each entry is keyed on the full image URL including its query string, and an entry older than the origin's `max-age` is treated as absent. *Amendment 2 (2026-10-10, DEC-952): the fill rule, not the cap, is what makes this a cache and not a mirror; 2 GB would hold about three quarters of the catalogue at `normal`.*
4. Requests carry an identifying `User-Agent` (`Eternities/<version> (+<repository URL>)`) and `Accept: image/*`; the image-path tests assert the outgoing `User-Agent`, because an engine's HTTP stack sends its own default unless one is set, and assert that the URL builder emits nothing outside the fixed path pattern over a validated UUID, because on the image hostnames a malformed path draws a 400 (probed 2026-10-10) and a 400 pauses every request. One concurrency limit applies: 6 in flight, counted across both hostnames. A 5xx response retries that URL with exponential backoff (1 s, 4 s, 16 s) and then leaves it for the session. A network failure retries the same way, but a URL left after network failures (every attempt a network failure; one 5xx among them leaves it for the session) becomes eligible again once any image request succeeds, so a dropped connection does not pin stands on their swatches until restart. While any URL is left after network failures, no pause is in force and no image request is in flight, the loader retries one such URL every 30 s, so a visitor who stands still gets images back without doing anything. A 429, a 403 or a 400 from either hostname pauses every image request, not only that URL, for at least 30 s (longer if `Retry-After` says so), queues that URL again, doubles the pause as served on each repeat (the next pause is the longer of twice the last and its own `Retry-After`), and resumes with one request in flight until a request succeeds, then returns to six. A 429, 403 or 400 that arrives while a pause is in force, including from a request already in flight when the pause began, belongs to that pause and is not a repeat; its URL is queued again. The third such pause in a session is not served: the loader stops fetching for the rest of the session and stands show their swatches (an image already resident or on disk still shows, 7.4.3). A redirect the loader refuses (4.15.1) is final for that URL for the session. Any other 4xx is final for that URL for the session. There is never a retry storm. The web edition's `docs/scryfall-policy.md` was re-verified for a desktop client on 2026-10-10 (DEC-952, §11 of that document), a 9.5 prerequisite. *Amendment 2 (2026-10-10, DEC-952): the first print backed off per URL, with retries at 1 s, 5 s and 21 s that all fall inside the 30 s for which Scryfall limits access after a 429, and treated a 403 as a missing image; a missing image is a 404.* *Amendment 2 erratum (2026-10-10, DEC-954 review notes N2 and N5 to N8): 400 joined the pause, because Scryfall's Access FAQ puts 400 beside 403 for its static files and a 400 is a statement about the client's request, not about one image; the pause, repeat and probe readings above are the ones the slice builds; a network failure no longer leaves a URL for the session.* *Amendment 2 erratum 2 (2026-10-10, DEC-955 review notes N10 to N12): the idle retry every 30 s, so a visitor who stands still recovers from a network drop; the URL-builder test, because on the image hostnames a 400 answers a malformed path (the first erratum's reason, rejected headers, describes the API host); and four readings the sentence left open are now in its text: the URL that drew the pause is queued again, the pause doubles as served, only a URL whose every attempt was a network failure returns, and a resident image stays after the stop.*
5. **Attribution.** Only complete card images are shown, and a complete card carries its artist line in the printed frame. In addition, card view shows the artist as text beside the printings panel (5.10.3) and the stand's hover label shows the artist (5.5.3). A screenshot therefore always contains the printed credit of every visible card image; a stand showing its swatch shows no image and needs no credit. The image surface, on a stand and in card view, takes its aspect from the image's pixel dimensions (488 × 680 and 672 × 936, about 5:7), never a nominal ratio, so the image is not stretched. *(Amendment 2 note: Scryfall's "do not distort, skew, or stretch" guideline; a 5:7 surface would stretch a `normal` image by 0.5 percent.)*

### 4.16 Lore tables (hero entries)

*Amendment 4 (2026-10-12, owner decision on DEC-950): by the owner's choice, every hero entry is laid out from three curated lore tables, and all twenty table sets are accepted before the first distribution. Appendix F has the schema and the rules in full; this subsection is the data contract.*

1. **Source form.** One directory per hero entry, `pipeline/data/lore/<slug>/`, with three JSON files: `regions.json` (the plane's places: 4 to 12 rows, each with a kit or variant, a rough position, a weight, its water, one sourced sentence and its sources), `eras.json` (the plane's history in lore order: 3 to 16 rows, or 3 to 24 for the Blind Eternities' product table, each with its sets, its region, its headline cards, one to three paragraphs whose every sentence carries its source, and the owner's acceptance record), and `keywords.json` (words of the lore that name a region). JSON, not YAML, because the C# decoder of the slice reads the tables as the pipeline does, with no extra dependency. The Appendix C.1 row of a hero entry names the directory in its `lore` field.
2. **Validation** (every pipeline run, failing the build with the row named): ids are unique slugs; anchors lie inside the unit disc (u² + v² ≤ 1) and are pairwise at least 0.15 apart; weights lie in [0.5, 2.0]; at most three distinct kits across an entry's regions (11.18); every region's kit, variant, water and ambient are values 4.12.2 and `kits.yaml` allow; every set with first printings on the plane appears in exactly one era, and an era names at least one set; every era's region exists; every headline card name is first printed in one of the era's sets; one to three paragraphs per era, one to six sentences per paragraph, at most 40 words per sentence and 300 per era; **every sentence has a source** on the allowlist of F.4; the presentation lint of 9.1.6 passes on every sentence; every keyword is at least three characters and names an existing region; every file carries exactly the fields of Appendix F.0 with their types; the cross-file identity checks of F.0 pass; for a product table, each era's sets lie in its region's family list and the families cover the plane's set codes exactly once; every source is validated by parsing its locator (F.4); and an `accepted` digest, when present, matches the files (F.2).
3. **Emission.** The resolved tables are emitted into the engine pack as `lore/<slug>.json` (4.13.2): eras with their text, years, region, sets, headline stand indices and sources; regions with their name, sentence and sources; the keyword list. The region cells and the trail are geometry and live in `worlds/<slug>.json` (4.13.5). The engine never carries a second copy of a table.
4. **Retained field.** The pipeline retains Scryfall's `story_spotlight` flag per printing (an amendment to v3 4.8). 5.13.2 uses it for an era's headline cards.
5. **The distribution flag.** A tagged build runs the pipeline with `--distribution`. Under it, every `hero: true` row (exactly the twenty of Appendix C.1, asserted) must have a lore directory that validates and whose `eras.json` carries an `accepted` record whose digest matches the files (F.2); otherwise the build fails naming the entry. The build script refuses to package a distribution archive from a pack whose manifest does not record `distribution: true`, so the check runs at pack build and again at packaging (8.9.1). Without the flag (a development build), a hero entry whose tables validate is laid out from them whether or not they are accepted, and a stale or absent `accepted` record is reported, not fatal; a hero entry with no lore directory is laid out by the kit law (the release-order reading of 5.4) and the report lists it under "hero entries without tables"; tables that fail validation fail the build in either mode. So development never waits on acceptance, and no distribution ships without it.
6. **Provenance.** Each sentence's source is one locator of the five forms of Appendix F.4: a page under `magic.wizards.com`, `scryfall.com` or `mtg.wiki`, validated by parsing the host; `card:<oracle_id>` for a card's name and type line; `printing:<scryfall_id>#front` or `#back` for a printing's printed flavour text. A source supports a sentence; it is never copied: the MTG Wiki's text is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (read 2026-10-12) and would bind ours, and Wizards' story text is theirs. The reviewer's check (5.13.4) is for support and for independent wording against the source the sentence names, and the visitor sees the sources in the reading stone's fold (5.13.1). One source per sentence makes the check possible and is not a licence determination (F.4).

## 5. The world

### 5.1 Principles

1. **Game-grade place.** The reference is a current third-person 3D game's environment art, not data visualisation. Real terrain, real water, real sky, real sound.
2. **The cards are exhibits.** Every card image is shown as a complete card, flat, upright, undistorted, untinted by lighting, exposure, fog or post-processing, and sharp. The world is lit; the cards are not. 8.4.4 names the rendering path that makes this true and the tests that check it.
3. **Never static.** Water flows, grass and trees move, clouds drift, particles rise, the avatar idles. Stillness happens only where the user asks for it (card view, reduced motion).
4. **One law for every world, read two ways.** Plaza, trail, sites, clusters, paths, pads and stones follow 5.4 on every world, so a visitor who has learned one world can read them all. On a hero world the trail runs through the plane's history in the lore's order and the regions are the lore's places (Appendix F); on a kit world the trail runs through the plane's sets in release order on one biome. Colour is read on the stand and in the HUD, never on the ground (5.5.1).
5. **Constrained freedom.** The avatar can go anywhere on the terrain and nowhere off it. World edges are natural (sea, cliffs, mist, a void) and never an invisible wall at a visible edge.

### 5.3 The hub

1. The application opens in the hub after the start screen. The hub is a compact void under a slow starfield, lit cold, on the `void` kit with its own hero set-piece: the **portal ring**.
2. **88 portals** stand on a ring of radius 56 m around the arrival point, 4.0 m apart centre to centre (circumference 352 m), one per plane in Appendix A including the Blind Eternities. Order: clockwise from north by the plane's first appearance year, ties by slug; empty planes last, alphabetical by slug. Each portal has a 3 m ring, a 0.5 m frame on each side (envelope 4 m, so neighbours touch at the frame and never overlap) and a 3 m pad in front of it toward the centre. Portal height scales with the log of the plane's card count in three steps; an empty plane's portal is small and dim but present (invariant 6). A waystone beside each pad shows the plane's name and card count.
3. **Preload.** At most one world preloads at a time: the one whose portal pad the avatar is nearest within 10 m. Changing the nearest portal cancels the previous preload after a 1 s hysteresis. Stepping into a portal whose world is not loaded uses the hold of 5.9.2.
4. The hub holds no stands. The Blind Eternities' 4,204 cards stand in the Blind Eternities' own world, reached through its portal like any plane; that world uses the `void` kit at world scale (Appendix C.1).
5. The hub has the travel menu (6.6), search and settings. The map in the hub shows the portal ring. Esc from any world returns to the hub through the return portal.

### 5.4 The world layout law and its allocator

*Amendment 4 (2026-10-12, owner decision on DEC-950 after the slice): this section replaces the first print's law in full. The first print's law, "colour is the wedge, time is the radius, each set is a site", produced a wheel that the owner judged a chart on the first slice (plan §13; DEC-953, question 3: no). Its machinery is kept: the global lattice, the jitter, the reservations, the independent checker, integer millimetres, the bounded loops and the named failure modes. Its geometry is withdrawn: there are no wedges, bands or rings, and colour is a cue on the stand (5.5.1), never ground. Draft 2 of the amendment (2026-10-12, after Codex round 5, plan §14.5) rewrote 5.4.1 to 5.4.15 and added 5.4.18.*

The law a visitor learns: **the trail runs through the plane's history; each era is a site; the cards of an era stand in small groups beside the site's winding path; colour is on the stand, not on the ground.** On a hero world the eras and their places come from the plane's lore tables (Appendix F): the regions you cross are the lore's places and the trail visits the eras in the lore's order. On a kit world the "eras" are the plane's sets in release order and the place is one biome. The allocator below makes the law a terminating, deterministic procedure; the pipeline runs it and the engine only reads the result. The Python implementation is the reference; 5.4.18 fixes the arithmetic so that the C# mirror reproduces it integer for integer; 5.4.15 states what has been computed so far and what has not.

**5.4.1 Constants.** All lengths are integer millimetres and all angles integer micro-degrees (5.4.18); the metre figures below are the stored integers divided by 1,000. Lattice pitch p = 3.0 m on the integer hexagonal lattice of 5.4.18 (nearest neighbours exactly 3.0 m apart); jitter radius j = 0.25 m, so any two stands are at least p − 2j = 2.5 m apart. Stand footprint 0.7 × 0.3 m; apron 1.0 m wide × 1.5 m deep on the side the image surface faces; corridor width 1.5 m. Path width 3 m (half width 1.5 m), for the trail and for every site path. Pad: a disc of **radius 1.5 m**. Stone: a disc of **radius 0.5 m**. Plaza: a disc of radius 12 m at the world origin with an edge path of width 3 m outside it (outer radius 15 m). Cluster: centre offset d = 6.0 m from the path's centreline; cluster disc radius ρ = 4.5 m; slot spacing along the path s = 9.0 m, sides alternating; size cap k drawn per slot from {3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8}. Site path: sweep spacing h = 21.0 m (= 2(d + ρ)); chord shortening 4.5 m at both ends of every sweep, except 1.5 m at the head end of sweep 0; half-turn radius h ⁄ 2; sweep wobble amplitude 3.0 m, wavelength drawn from [24 m, 40 m], tapered to zero over the 6 m nearest each chord end; a sweep shorter than s after shortening is dropped. Site blob: base radius r_s = max(9 m, √(A_site × n ⁄ π)) with A_site = 36 m² per stand; boundary deformation within ±20 percent (5.4.7); **site bounding radius b_s = 1.2 r_s + 9 m**, which contains the blob, the whole site path with its wobble and half-turns, the pad and the stone (5.4.8). Region: anchors inside the unit disc and pairwise at least 0.15 apart in table units; usable cell margin 10 m; site centre lattice pitch 10 m; minimum spacing between site bounding discs 6 m. Trail: routed on a 5 m grid with obstacles expanded by 5 m (5.4.11); wobble amplitude 3.0 m, wavelength from [30 m, 50 m], tapered to zero over the 10 m nearest each end of a leg; attachment segments of at most 10 m at each end. World: initial radius R₀ (5.4.4), floor 30 m; widening step 10 percent; at most 20 widenings per site; at most 20 outer passes. Prop clearance 1 m around paths, corridors, aprons, pads and stones; water buffer 3 m around the same. The world disc is a regular 72-gon and a blob is sampled at 72 vertices. PRNG: splitmix64, seeded with FNV-1a 64 of the string `eternities-v4|<plane slug>|<site key>` per site and `eternities-v4|<plane slug>|world` for world-level draws; every draw is listed, in order, in the subsection that takes it, and a site's stream is reset to its seed whenever that site is recomputed (5.4.9); the world stream is reset at the start of every outer pass and every restart (5.4.12). Python and C# implement the same integer procedure and are asserted against the same fixtures (5.4.14), and the invariant checks of 3.2 are run on the emitted positions regardless.

**5.4.2 Sites and their order.** On a hero world: one **era site** per row of the era table, in the table's order; then one **regional site** per region that receives at least one card under the keyword rule of 5.4.3, in region-table order. On a kit world: one **set site** per set with first printings on the plane, in release order, ties by set code. The plaza is not a site. A site's key is `era:<id>`, `region:<id>` or `set:<code>`; the key seeds its PRNG. This order is the **site order** and the trail visits the sites in it (5.4.11).

**5.4.3 Assignment of cards to sites.** Kit world: every card goes to the site of its first-printing set. Hero world: (a) the card's **era** is the era whose `sets` list carries the card's first-printing set code; the table maps every set code with first printings on the plane exactly once, otherwise the build fails naming the set (Appendix F.2); (b) if the card's name on any face contains a **keyword** of the keyword table as a whole word (case-insensitive, Unicode case-folded, word boundaries at spaces, punctuation and the string's ends), the card's **selected keyword** is the first matching keyword in table order; if the selected keyword's region differs from the era's region, the card goes to the **regional site** of that region; otherwise, and when no keyword matches, the card goes to its era's site. Within an era site cards are ordered by **chapter**: the era's sets in release order (ties by code), then by `oracle_id` bytes; a chapter is empty when every card of its set went to a regional site, and an empty chapter has no stone and no stands. Within a regional site or a set site: by first-printing set release order, then by `oracle_id` bytes. The report lists every keyword assignment, every card whose name matches keywords of two regions, and every empty chapter.

**5.4.4 Initial radius.** With b_s each site's bounding radius (5.4.1), R₀ = max(30 m, isqrt(2 × Σ_s b_s²) + 25 m): the site bounding discs fill at most half the world disc, and the plaza with its edge path (15 m) and one cell margin (10 m) are added. R₀ is a start, not a claim: the outer iteration (5.4.12) widens R when the regions, the sites or the trail do not fit, and only the fixtures state a world's size (5.4.15).

**5.4.5 Regions (the partition).** Kit world: one region, the whole world disc, unnamed. Hero world: each region row's anchor (u, v) lies inside the unit disc (u² + v² ≤ 1, validated by 4.16.2) and is scaled to (0.8 R u, 0.8 R v), so every anchor lies inside the world disc at a distance of at most 0.8 R from the origin; the disc is partitioned by the **power diagram** of the anchors with weights w_i = weight_i × (0.25 R)²: the cell of region i is the set of points x with |x − a_i|² − w_i ≤ |x − a_j|² − w_j for every j, clipped to the world polygon. Each cell is computed by half-plane intersection in region-table order in exact rational arithmetic and rounded once to integer millimetres (5.4.18); the cells are convex polygons that tile the disc to within the rounding. Because the anchors and the weights both scale with R, the partition's shape is the same at every R: a region whose cell is **empty** is a data error that no widening cures, and the build fails on the first pass naming the region ("empty cell; move the anchor or change the weight"). A non-empty cell grows with R². Each region's **usable cell** is its cell offset inward by the 10 m margin; the margin belongs to the trail. The region's row names its kit or variant and its water; the kit dresses the cell (5.6.3), and the pipeline emits the cell polygon so the engine never recomputes the partition.

**5.4.6 Site placement in a region.** Sites are placed in site order, each in its region (an era site in its era's region; a regional site in its region; on a kit world every site in the one region). Candidate centres are the points of a square lattice of pitch 10 m anchored at the origin that lie inside the usable cell. A candidate is **admissible** if the site's bounding disc (radius b_s) lies inside the usable cell, outside the plaza's edge path (outer radius 15 m), outside every hero volume, and at least 6 m from every already placed site's bounding disc. Among admissible candidates the allocator takes the one **nearest the previous site's pad** in site order (for the first site, nearest the plaza's edge path; for a regional site, nearest the pad of the last era site placed), ties by lower lattice row then column, so consecutive sites are neighbours and the trail's legs are short. The pad's position is known only after 5.4.8; placement uses the previous site's centre instead, which is within b_s of its pad. If no candidate is admissible the region is **short**, and the outer iteration widens R (5.4.12); a region still short on the twentieth pass fails the build naming the region and the site. Because every non-empty cell grows with R² while a site's bounding disc does not change with R, a region with a non-empty cell and at least one site becomes admissible for a large enough R; the pass bound, not the geometry, is the stopping rule, and a world that needs more than twenty passes fails naming the region and the site. Because every bounding disc lies outside every hero volume and outside the plaza, no hero volume and no part of the plaza ever cuts a blob, a path, a pad or a stone: hero volumes move sites, they do not clip them.

**5.4.7 Site blob.** The site's blob is a star-shaped polygon about its centre c_s: r(θ) = r_s × (1 + 0.2 × (a₂ cos(2θ + φ₂) + a₃ cos(3θ + φ₃) + a₄ cos(4θ + φ₄)) ⁄ (a₂ + a₃ + a₄)), with a_m drawn as integers in [200, 1000] thousandths and φ_m as integers in [0, 360,000,000) micro-degrees by the site PRNG in the order a₂, φ₂, a₃, φ₃, a₄, φ₄, sampled at 72 equally spaced θ (every 5°) into integer millimetres, so 0.8 r_s ≤ r(θ) ≤ 1.2 r_s. Nothing clips the blob (5.4.6). Stands may stand only inside the blob.

**5.4.8 Site path (the sweeps), pad and stone.** The site's path is a serpentine of **sweeps**. The sweep direction θ_s is drawn from [0, 180,000,000) micro-degrees by the site PRNG (one draw); d_s is its unit direction and n_s its left normal. Let z(v) = ⟨v − c_s, n_s⟩ over the blob's vertices, z_min and z_max its extremes and w = z_max − z_min the blob's extent across the sweeps. The number of sweeps is m = max(1, ⌊w ⁄ h⌋), at offsets c_k = z_min + (w − (m − 1) h) ⁄ 2 + k h for k = 0 … m − 1, so the family is centred on the **extent**, not on c_s, and every sweep line lies at least h ⁄ 2 inside the extent. Sweep k's **chord** is the longest segment of the line {x : z(x) = c_k} inside the blob (ties by the lower ⟨x, d_s⟩ of its start); sweeps are traversed alternately, sweep 0 in the direction d_s from the end with the lower ⟨x, d_s⟩, sweep 1 against it, and so on. Each chord is shortened by 4.5 m at both ends, except the **head end** of sweep 0 (where travel enters the site), which is shortened by 1.5 m. The chord of sweep 0 after shortening is the path's first run; its head is the path's **head**. **Joins.** Sweeps k and k + 1 are joined at the side where sweep k ends: let e_k be sweep k's far end and e'_{k+1} sweep k + 1's head end, and let t = min(⟨e_k, d_s⟩, ⟨e'_{k+1}, d_s⟩) when sweep k runs along d_s (max when it runs against it); both chords are **trimmed to t** on that side (trimming only shortens a chord and keeps it inside the blob), and the join is the half-turn: a 180° arc of radius h ⁄ 2 centred at the midpoint of the two trimmed ends, sampled every 15°, bulging outward beyond t by h ⁄ 2. A chord shorter than s after shortening and trimming is dropped with every sweep after it, and the path ends at the previous sweep's far end. **Wobble.** Each sweep's run is sampled every 0.5 m of its length and each sample is displaced along n_s by A(u) × sin(2π u ⁄ λ_k + ψ_k), where u is the distance from the run's head end, λ_k is drawn as integer millimetres in [24,000, 40,000] and ψ_k in [0, 360,000,000) micro-degrees (two draws per sweep, in sweep order), and A(u) is 3 m, tapered linearly to 0 over the 6 m nearest each end of the run, so the run's ends are preserved and the half-turns connect exactly. Half-turns are not wobbled. **Pad and stone.** The pad is a disc of radius 1.5 m centred 1.5 m before the head along the reversed direction of sweep 0, so the head lies on the pad's edge and the path begins there; because the head is 1.5 m inside the blob, the pad's centre lies on the blob's boundary and its outer half outside the blob, where the trail arrives (5.4.11). The site's stone (5.4.16) is a disc of radius 0.5 m beside the pad, its centre 2.5 m from the pad's centre along +n_s or −n_s, so it clears the pad by 0.5 m, the path's head by more than 1 m and an attachment strip from behind the pad by 0.5 m; which side is decided after the trail is routed: the side farther from the leg's attachment cell (5.4.11), ties +n_s. **Arc length** t along the path is the polyline length from the pad's edge through every run and half-turn. **Containment.** Every point of the path's centreline lies within 1.2 r_s + 6 m of c_s (a run lies inside the blob and is displaced at most 3 m; a half-turn's apex lies h ⁄ 2 − 4.5 m = 6 m beyond chord ends that lie inside the blob); with the path's half width, the pad and the stone, everything the site owns lies within b_s = 1.2 r_s + 9 m of c_s, and the checker asserts it on the emitted geometry. A site whose path is empty (sweep 0 dropped) fails the build naming the site; it cannot happen for r_s ≥ 9 m and is listed as a failure mode for the checker's sake.

**5.4.9 Clusters, candidates and capacity.** Cluster **slots** lie on the path's centreline at arc lengths s, 2s, 3s, … up to the path's length, sides alternating, the first on the left of the direction of travel from the pad; a slot that falls on a half-turn takes the turn's **inner** side (its cluster disc, centred h ⁄ 2 − d = 4.5 m from the turn's centre with radius ρ = 4.5 m, reaches exactly the path's inner edge at h ⁄ 2 − 1.5 m = 9 m), and the alternation continues as if the slot had taken its turn. For slot i the **cluster centre** is the point at distance d from the centreline along the normal on the slot's side, displaced by the site PRNG by an integer in [−1,500, 1,500] mm along the path's direction and an integer in [−1,000, 1,000] mm across it, followed by the slot's cap draw k_i from {3, …, 8} (three draws per slot, in slot order, void or not, so the draw sequence is fixed). A centre whose disc of radius ρ does not lie inside the blob makes the slot **void**; the alternation continues. For a non-void slot, its **candidates** are the points of the global hexagonal lattice (5.4.18, so stands in neighbouring clusters and sites keep the pitch between them) at most ρ − j from the centre that pass four tests, each on geometry **inflated by j on every side**: (a) the point's disc of radius j lies inside the blob; (b) the footprint centred on it, oriented so the image surface faces the nearest point of this site's path centreline (ties by the lower arc length), lies outside every reservation a footprint may not touch (this site's path and pad; the plaza and its edge path; hero volumes; the trail and other sites' geometry never enter a blob, 5.4.6 and 5.4.11, and the stones are tested at placement); (c) its apron lies outside the same; (d) its corridor, the straight 1.5 m strip from the apron's front edge to that nearest path point, lies inside the blob and outside every hero volume and the plaza. Candidates are enumerated in a fixed order: by the arc length of their path point, then by distance to the path, then by lattice (row, column). A cluster with fewer than 3 candidates is **void**, except that on a site with fewer than 3 stands the first slot whose candidate count is at least the site's stand count is kept. A cluster's **capacity** is min(k_i, its candidate count); the site's capacity is the sum over its clusters, less one per chapter stone the site needs (5.4.10). If the capacity is below the site's stand count, the site is **widened**: its widening count is incremented, r_s is multiplied by 1.1, its PRNG is reset to its seed, and 5.4.7 to 5.4.9 are recomputed for that site; its larger bounding disc is re-checked against 5.4.6, and if it no longer fits, the outer iteration widens R (5.4.12). After 20 widenings of one site without reaching capacity the build fails naming the site. Capacity counts candidates independently; conflicts between chosen stands are resolved at placement (5.4.10) and the chosen set is validated afterwards, not assumed.

**5.4.10 Placement.** Placement runs after every site of the world has capacity and the trail is reserved (5.4.12). Sites are placed in site order; within a site, clusters in slot order from the pad; within a cluster, candidates in their fixed order. For each candidate the site PRNG draws its **jitter** (an angle in [0, 360,000,000) micro-degrees and a radius in [0, 250] mm, two draws per candidate in candidate order, accepted or not), the candidate is displaced by it with its yaw unchanged, and the displaced footprint, apron and corridor are tested **directly, without inflation**, against: every reservation of 5.4.11 (the exclusions of invariant 9 by geometry type); the footprint and apron of every stand accepted so far in this or any earlier site; the corridor of every stand accepted so far (a footprint or apron may not lie in a corridor; two corridors may cross only at an angle of 30° or more); every stone of this site, including the chapter stones placed so far; and the containment rules (the footprint inside the blob and inside its cluster's disc). A candidate that passes is **accepted** at its displaced position; because the test is on the final geometry, acceptance is itself the proof of invariant 9 and the independent checker (3.2) repeats it. Acceptance in a cluster stops at its capacity and in the site at its stand count. **Partial clusters.** A cluster that accepts fewer than 3 stands is permitted only as the site's last cluster or on a site with fewer than 3 stands; a cluster that would otherwise close under 3 **exhausts** the site. If the site's clusters are exhausted before its stand count is reached, the site is widened as in 5.4.9 and the pass **restarts** at step (4) of 5.4.12 with the widened r_s kept. **Order.** The site's cards, in their 5.4.3 order, are assigned to the accepted positions in acceptance order; because clusters are taken in slot order and candidates in arc-length order, an era's chapters run along the path from the pad outward, and the same data always yields the same picture. The yaw is the direction from the stand to its corridor's path point. **Chapter stones.** In an era site, each non-empty chapter after the first has a chapter stone: it is placed as the first accepted candidate of the chapter, a disc of radius 0.5 m on the candidate's lattice point without jitter, tested against the same exclusions as a footprint; it takes one unit of its cluster's capacity, and the chapter's first stand is the next accepted candidate. Two chapter stones can never coincide, because each occupies its own lattice point. After all sites of a world are placed, the independent checker (3.2) runs on the final geometry; a checker failure is a build failure naming the first violating pair and is treated as a defect in the allocator, never as a data condition and never silently retried.

**5.4.11 Reservations and the trail.** Reservations are polygons in integer millimetres, made in this order, each checked against the earlier ones under invariant 9: the plaza (a disc of radius 12 m at the origin) and its edge path (the annulus from 12 m to 15 m), holding the arrival point (facing north) and the return portal (8 m south of the centre, facing it) with its pad; the hero volumes from the owner's file (clipped to the world; a volume that covers the arrival point or the return pad fails the build naming the volume); then, for every site in site order, its path (a 3 m wide strip along the centreline polyline of 5.4.8, with the half-turns) and its pad. Then the **trail** is routed and reserved, and only then the stones (5.4.8) and the candidates (5.4.9, 5.4.10). **Legs.** The trail is a sequence of legs: from the plaza's edge path to the first site's pad; from each site's pad to the next site's pad in site order over the era sites (hero world) or the set sites (kit world); and a **spur** from the trail so far to each regional site's pad, in site order. **Grid and obstacles.** Routing is on the square grid of pitch 5 m anchored at the origin; a cell is **blocked** when its square intersects any of: the outside of the world polygon; a hero volume; the plaza with its edge path; any site's blob; each of these **expanded by 5 m**, which exceeds the leg's half width plus its wobble. Other legs are not obstacles (a crossing is a junction); pads and stones are inside their site's expanded blob and need no rule of their own. **Ends.** A leg's two **anchor points** are: for the plaza, the point of the edge path's outer boundary nearest the first site's pad centre; for a site, its pad centre; for a spur's start, the point of the trail so far (sampled every 0.5 m) nearest the regional site's pad centre. A leg's **attachment cell** at an end is the unblocked cell whose centre is nearest that anchor point among cells whose centre lies within 10 m of it and, for a site's pad, behind the pad (⟨x − pad centre, d_s⟩ ≤ 0, so the trail arrives from outside the blob, opposite the path's head) (ties by lower row then column); if there is none, the leg has no route. The grid route runs between the two attachment cells by A* with eight-neighbour moves at integer costs 5 (straight) and 7 (diagonal), the octile distance as the heuristic, no diagonal move past a blocked orthogonal neighbour, ties on f by the lower (row, column) of the cell being expanded, and a predecessor replaced only on a strictly lower g; a cell whose centre is within 10 m of a blob costs double, so the trail skirts sites. **Smoothing** runs once along the route from its start: a vertex is removed whenever the straight segment between its neighbours intersects no blocked cell's square. **Attachment.** Each end of the smoothed route is joined to its anchor point by a straight **attachment segment** from the attachment cell's centre to the nearest point of the anchor's pad edge (or of the edge path's outer boundary, or the spur's trail point); the attachment segment is exempt from the blob expansion, and the checker tests its strip against every exclusion of invariant 9. **Wobble.** The route between the attachment segments is sampled every 0.5 m and displaced along its left normal by A(u) × sin(2π u ⁄ λ + ψ), λ drawn as integer millimetres in [30,000, 50,000] and ψ in [0, 360,000,000) micro-degrees from the world PRNG (two draws per leg, in leg order), with A(u) = 3 m tapered linearly to 0 over the 10 m nearest each end. The leg, its attachment segments included, is reserved as a 3 m wide strip. **Failure.** A leg with no route (no attachment cell at an end, or A* exhausts the grid) makes the world **short**, and the outer iteration widens R (5.4.12); a leg still without a route on the twentieth pass fails the build naming its two ends. Water crosses the trail or a site path only at a ford or bridge prefab (8.4.2); the water buffer and the prop clearance of 5.4.1 apply to every reservation here.

**5.4.12 Order of computation, outer iteration and termination.** For one world: (1) sites and assignment (5.4.2, 5.4.3); (2) R₀ (5.4.4); then, with R: (3) regions (5.4.5); (4) site centres (5.4.6); (5) blobs (5.4.7); (6) site paths and pads (5.4.8); (7) candidates and capacity for every site (5.4.9), widening a short site in place and repeating (5) to (7) for that site until it has capacity, fits, or fails; (8) reservations and the trail (5.4.11), then the site stones; (9) placement and chapter stones (5.4.10); (10) the checker (3.2). **Restart.** A site exhausted at step (9) is widened and the pass restarts at step (4): every site centre, blob, path, reservation and placement of the pass is discarded; the widened r_s and every site's widening count are kept; every site's PRNG and the world PRNG are reset to their seeds. **Widening R.** If a region is short (5.4.6), a widened site no longer fits (5.4.9) or a leg has no route (5.4.11), R is multiplied by 1.1 and a new pass starts at step (3), discarding everything after step (2), keeping the widening counts. **Bounds.** At most 20 passes over R; at most 20 widenings per site; each restart at step (4) is caused by a widening, so there are at most 20 × (number of sites) restarts in a pass; A* expands each cell at most once; the sweep count is at most the blob's extent over h; every loop therefore has an explicit bound and the allocator terminates. **Failure modes**, each failing the build with its subject named: a set with first printings and no era, or two eras (5.4.3, Appendix F.2); a keyword naming an unknown region (Appendix F.3); an empty region cell (5.4.5); a region that cannot hold its sites after 20 passes (5.4.6); a site with an empty path (5.4.8); a site that cannot reach capacity after 20 widenings (5.4.9, 5.4.10); a trail leg with no route after 20 passes (5.4.11); a hero volume covering the arrival point or the return pad (5.4.11); and an independent-checker violation on the final geometry (5.4.10), which the procedure as written cannot produce and which is therefore an allocator defect.

**5.4.13 Small worlds.** A world with few cards has the plaza, one site whose blob has the floor radius of 9 m, one sweep and one or two clusters, and R of about 70 m, so about 140 m across before the terrain margin: a clearing at the end of a short trail. A one-card plane (Shandalar) shows one stand beside one short path from its pad; its marker stone says "1 card". A two-card plane (Vryn) has one set site with one cluster of two, kept under the small-site rule of 5.4.9. A four-card plane (Zhalfir) has one set site whose first cluster's cap may be 3, in which case the fourth stand is in the second cluster; if the floor-radius blob yields one slot, the widening of 5.4.9 adds the second. The fixtures decide the exact figures.

**5.4.14 Fixtures.** `contract/test-vectors/v4/layout/` holds the allocator's complete output (regions, site centres and blobs, paths, pads, stones, the trail, reservations, every stand position, cluster index, corridor point and yaw) for: Dominaria with its accepted lore tables (6,271 cards, 37 set codes with first printings); the Blind Eternities with its product tables (4,204 cards, 50 set codes); Rabiah (75 cards, one set, the kit law); Zhalfir (4); Vryn (2); Shandalar (1); a synthetic hero plane with one region and one era (its site count equals the kit law's one set site when it has one set); a synthetic hero plane whose era table leaves one set unmapped (must fail naming the set); a synthetic keyword that moves exactly one card to a regional site; a synthetic region whose weight empties its cell (must fail naming the region); a synthetic region whose anchor lies outside the unit disc (must fail validation); a synthetic era of three sets of one card each (three chapter stones at three distinct lattice points in one cluster); and Dominaria with a synthetic hero volumes file that occupies part of one region, so that a site moves and a trail leg routes around it. Synthetic fixtures run under the pipeline's `--fixture` flag, which relaxes the row minimums of Appendix F (4 regions, 3 eras) and the `accepted` requirement and nothing else. The Python implementation produces them; the C# mirror must reproduce them exactly (integer millimetres and micro-degrees); and the invariant checks of 3.2 are run on every fixture by a third, independent checker that knows nothing of the allocator. Until Dominaria's tables are accepted (8.12.7), the Dominaria fixtures run on the schema example of Appendix F.5 and are labelled so in the report.

**5.4.15 What has been computed, and what has not.** Nothing of this law has been run on real data. From the constants and the R₀ formula alone, on dataset `a5846bec28d1447e`, with the schema example's era grouping for Dominaria and the kit law's set sites standing in for the other hero entries' unwritten tables: Dominaria about 1,100 m across; the Blind Eternities' world about 950 m; Ravnica about 700 m; Innistrad about 600 m; Rabiah about 180 m; one-to-four-card worlds about 140 m (the plaza forces three widenings of the 106 m start). These are the formula's **starting radii before any widening**, not fixtures, and the real figures can only be larger; the first print's estimate of about 480 m was wrong (plan §14.5, F55). What sets the size is the site bounding discs (b_s² summed, times two): a 600-card era needs a disc about 100 m across, and Dominaria's fifteen eras need fifteen of them. For comparison, the first print's law on the first slice gave Dominaria 824 m across with 28 of its 36 bands at the 6 m minimum, 5,955 of 6,271 stands facing a ring path and 610 stands with no neighbour within 8 m (DEC-976, plan §13.3). The difference in kind: the journey law's size is dressed terrain between neighbouring sites with a road through it, not empty bands of stands; the chain placement of 5.4.6 keeps consecutive eras adjacent so that a leg is of the order of a site's width; and travel never requires walking (5.9.4). The allocator leg of the second slice (9.6) implements this law in C# and reports Dominaria's actual figures, with the trail's leg lengths and the walking time from the plaza to the last era; the pipeline implementation in Wave 5 must reproduce them. Risk 22 names what the owner judges if the figures are too large.

**5.4.16 Site furniture.** The stone at a site's pad is a **reading stone** on an era site (5.13), a **waystone** on a regional site and on a set site of five or more stands, and a **marker stone** on a set site under five stands (name and year, no travel). Chapter stones stand at the first lattice point of each non-empty chapter after the first in an era site (set name and year, no travel; 5.4.10). The travel menu and the map list every site, so a marker-stone site is reachable without a waystone.

**5.4.17 The rows risk.** From the air a large site is a comb of sweeps 21 m apart. On the ground, what must make it read as a wood with paths: the wobble, so no run is straight; the clusters, so cards are met in groups and never in a line; the varying caps, so no two groups are alike; the 21 m between sweeps, dressed by the kit so a neighbouring sweep is behind trees, water or rock rather than in sight; and the blob shapes, no two alike. The second slice's first question (9.6) judges it on Dominaria. If it fails, the named alternative is a **branching path**: a tree grown from the pad by the same slot, cluster, capacity, widening and failure rules. It is a design to be written, not a switch: an amendment specifies it to the standard of this section and passes Codex before the one re-slice 9.6 allows (11.17). The map is the only place a whole site is drawn.

**5.4.18 Numerical rules.** These rules make two implementations produce the same integers. (1) **Units and types.** Lengths are int64 millimetres, angles int64 micro-degrees, areas int64 square millimetres, ratios int64 thousandths; no quantity is stored or compared as a floating-point number. (2) **Rounding.** Where a rule produces a non-integer, it is rounded once, half away from zero, at the point the rule names; intermediate products are exact integers or exact rationals. (3) **Square roots** are `isqrt`, the floor of the integer square root. (4) **Trigonometry.** sin and cos are read from one committed table, `contract/test-vectors/v4/layout/trig.bin`: for every integer k in [0, 360,000), sin and cos of k thousandths of a degree as int32 scaled by 2³⁰, generated once by the pipeline and never regenerated at build; an angle in micro-degrees is reduced modulo 360,000,000 and truncated to thousandths of a degree before the lookup. A length times a table value is divided by 2³⁰ with rounding (add 2²⁹, then floor-divide). (5) **The hexagonal lattice** has integer axial coordinates (i, k): x = 3,000 i + 1,500 k, z = 2,599 k millimetres; the nearest-neighbour distance is min(3,000, isqrt(1,500² + 2,599²)) = 3,000 mm, so the 2.5 m bound of 5.4.1 holds exactly with the jitter. (6) **Random draws.** splitmix64 yields 64 bits per draw; an integer in [lo, hi] is lo + (bits mod (hi − lo + 1)), the small modulo bias accepted and identical in both implementations; every drawn quantity is an integer in the units of (1). (7) **Polygons.** The power cells are computed in exact rational arithmetic (Python `fractions.Fraction`; C# `System.Numerics.BigInteger` numerators and denominators) and each vertex is rounded once; the rounded cells may overlap or gap by at most 1 mm along shared edges, and containment tests use the region's own rounded polygon. Blobs, paths, strips, discs (as 72-gons) and all other geometry are polygons with integer vertices. Point-in-polygon is the even-odd rule with the boundary counted inside; segment intersection uses integer cross products; distance comparisons compare squared distances in int64. (8) **Curves** are polylines: the blob at 72 vertices, half-turns every 15°, runs and legs sampled every 500 mm; arc length is the integer sum of segment lengths by isqrt. (9) **Overflow.** With R under 10 km every squared length is under 10¹⁴ and every product of two such under 10²⁸, which the rational arithmetic of (7) carries and which no int64 path reaches; implementations assert it. (10) **Reference.** The Python implementation is the reference; a C# result that differs on any fixture is a C# defect.

### 5.5 Stands

1. A stand is an image surface about 0.63 × 0.88 m (card aspect about 5:7; the surface takes the image's pixel aspect, 4.15.5) on a plinth about 1.1 m tall, facing its apron. It shows the first-printing `normal` image when the avatar is within 25 m, and the swatch colour beyond that. Rarity sets the plinth's material in four steps (stone, bronze, silver, gold-veined); the class sets a thin rim of the class's colour on the plinth's edge, in every kit and at every time of day, gold for multicolour and grey for colourless: this is the **colour cue**, the place colour lives now that it is not geography (Amendment 4; the first slice shipped without the rim, DEC-976). Rarity and class are metadata made visible, not tiers to earn.
2. The image surface is rendered on the isolated path of 8.4.4: unlit, exposure-independent, fog-free, excluded from post-processing, depth-tested against the world. Nothing about the image changes with the scene's lighting or weather. The plinth is lit normally and casts shadows; the image surface receives none.
3. Within 3 m the stand's hover label shows card name, set, year and artist. Interacting (6.1.3) enters card view.
4. Minimum stand spacing is 2.5 m (5.4.1). Each stand has a 1.5 m apron, the point a travel portal lands on, and a corridor to its site's path (5.4.9). Stands stand in clusters of one to eight, three to eight by design (5.4.9, 5.4.10), never in a row.
5. Image loading is nearest-first within the 25 m radius, bounded by the slot count of 8.4.3; the loader never requests more images than there are slots, so residency is a hard bound and 25 m is a radius within which images are preferred, not a promise. A stand whose image has not arrived or has no slot shows its swatch, never a placeholder rectangle. Images fade in over 200 ms.
6. Filtered-out stands (6.5) show their swatch, not a dimmed image, lose their rim glow and are not interactive. A filtered-out stand reached through search is un-filtered on arrival (6.4.6).

### 5.6 Environments: kits, theme rows, hero set-pieces

1. **Twelve kits** cover the roster: `temperate` (meadow, rivers, lakes), `forest`, `coast` (sea, islands, cliffs), `desert`, `volcanic` (lava, ash, basalt), `mountain` (steppe, snow line), `swamp`, `moor` (gothic, fog), `stone-city`, `filigree-city`, `phyrexian` (metal, oil), `void` (the hub, the Blind Eternities' world and the pockets). A kit is a terrain layer set, a water configuration, a vegetation or structure prop set, a sky and lighting rig, a weather set, particle effects and a sound set (5.12). The engineers own the kit code and prefabs; the kit assets are supplied as 8.12 says. Given a theme row, the layout from the pack and a seed, a kit dresses any world size.
2. **Theme rows** (Appendix C) tune a kit per plane: sky, light, palette, weather, water, ambient family, music mood. Two planes on the same kit with different rows read as siblings; two on different kits read as different worlds. A kit world whose row inherits every field from the kit reads as the kit's default; 11.8 schedules the curation.
3. **Region dressing.** On a hero world each region is dressed by the kit and variant its row names (Appendix F.1): ground layers, prop set and density, water, with a 20 m blend across the region boundary and a ford or bridge where water crosses the trail or a site path. A hero world names at most three distinct kits across its regions (11.18); one terrain carries them as per-region layer weights (8.4.5). On a kit world the kit varies its ground layer mix and prop density per site from the site's PRNG, so no two sites look the same. No dressing follows colour class: colour is the cue on the stand (5.5.1). The first print's per-plane wedge dressing (Alara's shards, Lorwyn's two aspects) is now the region table's job; 11.9 is resolved. *(Amendment 4.)*
4. **Hero set-pieces and lore tables.** The twenty entries in Appendix C.1 (nineteen planes and the Blind Eternities) carry two things. First, their lore tables (Appendix F), from which the pipeline lays the world out; by the owner's decision all twenty table sets are accepted before the first distribution (4.16.5, 8.12.7). Second, hand-built geometry by the owner in the editor: a landmark in the plaza and up to one landmark per region, placed by the owner inside a hero volume the owner exports (4.12.2) so the pipeline can keep stands, paths and the trail out; the region row's `set_piece` field is the proposal. Set-pieces are additive and staged: a hero entry whose set-piece scene is missing ships on its kit's dressing of its regions. The first release carries the hub's portal ring (core content) and Dominaria's set-piece; the other nineteen entries' set-pieces follow release by release (8.12.4). *(Amendment 4.)*
5. **Kit worlds.** The 26 planes with cards that are not hero entries ship on their kit and theme row alone, laid out by the journey law's release-order reading (5.4): one region, the sets as sites along the trail, no prose.
6. **Pockets.** The 42 empty planes share the generated `pocket` row: a void space about 20 m across with a single waystone that reads the plane's name and "no cards first printed here", and a return portal.
7. Adding a plane is one Appendix A row, one Appendix B row and one `themes.yaml` entry. The pipeline fails if any is missing.

### 5.7 Sky, light and weather

1. Each world has one sky and one lighting rig from its theme row: time of day, sun or moon elevation and tone, cloud cover, fog colour and density, and optional weather (rain, snow, ash, sandstorm haze, fireflies, embers). HDRP's physically based sky, volumetric fog and volumetric clouds are the implementation; the HDRP low quality level (8.1.1) replaces volumetric clouds with a cloud layer and reduces fog quality.
2. Time of day is fixed per world; there is no day cycle in v4. Clouds and weather move.
3. Weather never obscures a stand within the 3 m interaction radius, and never touches the image surface (5.1.2).

The visitor moves as a third-person avatar with a following camera (over the shoulder, with an orbit that follows the mouse with no button held, or the right stick, and a zoom range of 2 to 6 m; 6.1.2). *(Amendment 4: the first print said right-drag; the owner asked for no held button on the first slice, DEC-975.)* The avatar has idle, walk, run, turn-in-place, look-at-stand and portal-step animations, and foot IK on terrain.
2. v1 ships **one avatar: Teferi** (Appendix D). There is no avatar selection screen and no roster list in v1; the start screen says "You travel as Teferi." Adding avatars later adds the selection surface, which section 10 risk 1 treats as a game-read risk to be judged then.
3. The avatar is a camera subject. It has no stats, no abilities, no inventory, no health, no stamina bar and nothing to pick up. Walking is 3 m/s, running 6 m/s, with no cost.
4. The avatar's design is an original stylised interpretation of the character, made from the card art as reference, never a copy or projection of card art. The vertical slice uses a placeholder mannequin; Teferi's model is not commissioned before 11.1 is decided.
5. The avatar sets the home plane (Dominaria for Teferi): the visitor arrives in the hub facing that portal, and the travel menu has a "your cards" section (4.14.4).

### 5.9 Portals

1. A portal is a standing ring of about 3 m with a shader surface. Stepping into it plays a 1.5 s transition (surface fills the view, a sound, a cut). A hub portal's world begins loading per 5.3.3; a return portal's hub is always resident.
2. If the destination is not ready when the transition ends, the portal surface **holds** with input locked for up to the 6 s ceiling (7.2); beyond 6 s a loading indicator appears on the held surface. The transition never cuts to an unready world.
3. Every portal is two-way. A world's return portal stands in the plaza (5.4.11).
4. A **travel portal** opens 2 m in front of the avatar, facing it, wherever the avatar stands (a portal has no collision and needs no reserved ground); the avatar walks into it automatically over 1 s, with no steering by the visitor, and arrives on the destination stand's apron facing the stand, or on the destination pad facing the site's stone. The portal closes behind the avatar. For a destination in another world, the travel portal leads straight to that world's destination: the world loads behind the transition with the hold rule, and the hub is not visited.
5. Reduced motion (5.11) replaces the transition with a 0.3 s fade; the hold rule and the automatic walk still apply.

### 5.10 Card view

1. Interacting with a stand enters card view: the camera moves over 0.6 s to frame the card at a fixed on-screen size (about 60 percent of viewport height), slightly off-centre to leave room for the printings panel; the world stays behind the card with a subtle depth-of-field on the world only (never on the card); the avatar steps out of frame. Nothing in the world can occlude the card in card view: the camera is placed inside the stand's apron looking at the image surface, and props keep the 1 m clearance from aprons (5.4.1).
2. The card is the first-printing `large` image on a thin rounded-rectangle solid with the Scryfall card back and a dark edge, as v3 5.6.2, rendered on the isolated path (8.4.4). It tilts toward the pointer with spring damping, ±12°, and settles when the pointer leaves. Cards with the back-image flag (4.13.3) show a flip control.
3. The **printings panel** lists every included printing: set name, year, rarity, artist, ordered by release date, the active printing highlighted. Clicking a row swaps the card face. Above the list: card name, mana cost, type line, oracle text per face, colour identity and rarity as text. Below: "Open on Scryfall" (opens the system browser) and the artist credit for the shown image as text.
4. Esc, the back control or walking input leaves card view and returns the camera to the avatar over 0.6 s. The stand is added to recently visited (6.6.4).
5. Card view pauses nothing in the world: water flows, clouds drift, sound continues.

### 5.11 Motion and reduced motion

1. All motion is delta-time based and must look identical at 30, 60 and 120 fps.
2. With the system reduced-motion setting, or the setting in 6.9: camera transitions drop to 0.3 s fades, the following camera's smoothing is disabled, card tilt is disabled, weather particles stop, vegetation wind stops, water keeps a flat animated surface. Walking is unchanged; the travel menu is the travel aid.

### 5.12 Sound

Three layers plus interface sounds, all original or CC0 licensed with provenance recorded (8.12.3), never Wizards' audio (Fan Content Policy).

1. **Ambient bed.** One loop per ambient family in Appendix C (fourteen families), crossfaded at region boundaries on a hero world (a region row may name its own family, Appendix F.1; the default is the theme row's) so the marsh gains frogs and the coast gains surf; one family over a kit world. Stereo, 1 to 2 MB each as Ogg Vorbis.
2. **Music.** One mood per plane from Appendix C (fifteen moods). v4 ships a small set of original or CC0 ambient loops, one per mood, 2 to 4 minutes each, looped with a crossfade, ducked under card view. Generative music is deferred (11.3).
3. **Spatial prop sound.** Every water body, lava flow, fire, forge, machine, wind-exposed tree line and waterfall emits positional sound with distance attenuation and reverb zones (a cave sounds like a cave). A river is heard before it is seen. Implementation is standard 3D audio sources and reverb zones (8.6); a spatialiser plugin is an upgrade, not a dependency.
4. **Interface sounds.** Portal open, portal step, card view enter, card view leave, waystone use, travel menu open. Six sounds.
5. Master, ambient, music, effects and interface volumes are separate settings. Sound starts on the first input after the start screen.

### 5.13 The reading layer

*Amendment 4 (2026-10-12, owner decision on DEC-950): non-goal 9 is reversed for hero worlds, in this form and no other. The owner chose that an engineer drafts the text from named sources, a reviewer checks every sentence against its source, and the owner accepts the text.*

1. **Form.** A **reading stone** stands at each era site's pad (5.4.16): a standing stone of about 2 m with an inscription face. Within 3 m its hover label shows the era's name and years. Interacting (6.1.3) opens the **reading overlay**: the era's name, its years, its place (the region's name), its one to three paragraphs, its sets (name and year, each a travel link to the set's chapter), its headline cards (name and set, each a travel link to the stand), and a **Sources** fold listing every source the era's sentences and its region's sentence name (Appendix F.4). Esc, the back control or walking input closes it. Text only, no voice. It is an overlay (6.2.1), keyboard- and gamepad-operable (6.10.2), and the HUD's scrim rule applies (6.10.4). Opening it to text shown is a 7.2 row.
2. **Headline cards.** The Story Spotlight cards of the era's sets (Scryfall `story_spotlight`, retained per 4.16.4), in release order then collector number, up to six. If the era's sets have none, the era row's `headline` list (Appendix F.2). If that is empty, the first three legendary creatures of the era's sets by release date then name. The rule is in the pipeline; the overlay reads the result from the pack.
3. **Words.** Every sentence is ours, written from the sources it names (4.16.6); no sentence quotes Wizards' story text or copies the MTG Wiki; the presentation lint of 9.1.6 applies, so the words "game" and "player" do not appear; no sentence addresses the visitor or asks anything of them (goal 1; 9.3's last criterion). The text describes the plane's history and the cards that depict it; it does not describe the product, except on the Blind Eternities (item 8).
4. **Accuracy and review.** An engineer drafts each entry's tables on a content issue of its own (8.12.7). A second engineer, never the drafter, checks every sentence against the source it names for support and for independent wording, rejects any sentence the source does not support or that rewords the source's passage, and records in the verdict, per sentence, the passage relied on (Appendix F.4). The owner accepts the table set on that issue naming its digest, the `accepted` record is written into the table (Appendix F.2), and the pipeline verifies the digest on every run, so any later edit needs re-acceptance. A model's own recollection, without a source, is not a source.
5. **No mechanic.** No counter of stones read, nothing marks an era as "done", no trail completion, no reward. The recently-visited list stays a bookmark list (6.6.4).
6. **Kit worlds** have no reading stones. Their waystones and marker stones show the set's name, year and stand count; the plane's history there is its release order.
7. **Region sentence.** Crossing into a region shows its one sourced sentence in the HUD for 4 s (6.3.1); it is the only text shown outside the overlay.
8. **The Blind Eternities.** Its tables are `kind: product` (Appendix F.2): its regions are set families, each with its list of sets, and its eras are one family over one release period, so every card stands in its family's region (F.2); its stones describe the products that printed its cards, in our words, from Scryfall's and Wizards' set pages. Its reading stones read like a catalogue's notes, never a story.
9. **Scale.** Dominaria: about 10 eras and about 20 paragraphs, under 3,000 words. Twenty entries: about 150 eras. One entry's text is read in minutes, not hours.

## 6. Interaction requirements

### 6.1 Controls

1. Keyboard and mouse, and a standard gamepad. Both are complete; nothing requires one or the other.
2. Explore: WASD or left stick to walk, Shift or a trigger to run, mouse or right stick to orbit the camera, scroll or a shoulder button to zoom.
3. Interact (E, a face button, or a click while a stand's label is showing within 3 m; the click has no place of its own, DEC-975) enters card view, or uses a portal, a reading stone or a waystone.
4. Esc or the menu button: leave card view, close an overlay, or return to the hub through the return portal, in that order.
5. M opens the map; T opens the travel menu; `/` opens search; Tab shows the HUD legend; `?` opens help; F12 takes a screenshot.

### 6.2 Modes

1. Four modes: **start** (start screen), **hub**, **explore** (in a world or pocket) and **card view**. Overlays (map, travel menu, search, settings, help) sit over hub and explore.
2. Mode transitions are the portal transition (hub to world and back), the card view transition (0.6 s) and the travel portal. No cut is ever instantaneous except under reduced motion fades.

### 6.3 HUD

1. Top-left: plane name, the region you stand in (hero world) and the site (for example "Dominaria · Shiv · The Brothers' War"; on a kit world "Rabiah · Arabian Nights, 1993"), updated as you cross a boundary; crossing into a region shows the region's sentence beneath it for 4 s (5.13.7). In the hub: "The Hub".
2. Filter chips beneath the plane name, as v3 6.3.2, with a live count of matching stands.
3. The HUD legend (Tab) lists the present colour classes with their rim colours (5.5.1) and the absent ones; it is the key to the stands' rims and the map's dots. Colour is not a direction.
4. **Map (M):** a top-down overlay of the current world: regions as areas named at their anchors (hero world), the trail as a line with the sites' stones numbered in site order, sites as outlines with their chapter stones, the avatar, the return portal, and the stands that match the active filter as dots in their class colour. Clicking a site or a stone opens a travel portal to its pad; clicking a region opens one to the pad of its first site in site order, and a region with no site is named on the map and offers no travel. The map is a travel aid and shows nothing the world does not.
5. The HUD hides in card view except for the printings panel and the breadcrumb, and hides entirely for a screenshot (6.8).

### 6.4 Search

1. Opened by `/` or the search control. A text box over the scene; Esc or clicking outside dismisses it.
2. Matches card names, plane names and set names with fuzzy, prefix-favouring matching; double-faced cards match on either face name; avatar aliases match their cards.
3. Results are grouped Planes, Sets, Cards, up to 8 per group. Arrow keys move, Enter selects.
4. Selecting a plane opens a travel portal to its plaza. Selecting a card opens a travel portal to its stand's apron, in this world or another (5.9.4).
5. Selecting a set reads the set's stand locations from the pack (4.13.4, 4.13.6). If the set has stands, the travel portal leads to the pad of the site that holds most of them (on a hero world its era site, where the travel menu opened on arrival lists the set's chapter; on a kit world its set site; ties by plane index, then site index), and the travel menu lists the set's other sites by plane whether or not that site has a waystone. If the set has no sites (reprint-only), the set's filter chip is added, the avatar stays, and the map and a toast show how many stands match in this world and across worlds.
6. Search ignores active filters when matching. Selecting a filtered-out card clears exactly the facets that exclude it, with a toast "Filters cleared to show this card" and an undo chip, so the stand is interactive on arrival. There is no dead end.
7. The search index is in the data pack and is loaded before the start screen dismisses. No network per keystroke.

### 6.5 Filters

1. Facets, semantics and rarity mapping as v3 6.6.1 to 6.6.4, evaluated against `stands.bin` (colour mask, type mask, rarity) and `sets.bin` (set membership) so every facet is live in every world without a shard load. Filters apply to stands per 5.5.6 and to the map's dots.
2. Filter state persists for the session and is shown as chips. There are no URLs; a filter is cleared by its chip, by clear-all, or by 6.4.6.

### 6.6 Travel

1. **Travel menu (T).** A searchable list with sections: this world's sites in trail order (on a hero world the eras, each with its chapters, then the regional sites; on a kit world the sets); this world's regions (hero world); "your cards" for the avatar; recently visited; random. Choosing an entry opens a travel portal (5.9.4) to the site's pad, the chapter's stone (the site's pad for a first chapter; an empty chapter is not listed), the region's first site's pad or the stand's apron.
2. **Stones.** Using a reading stone or a waystone offers the previous and the next site on the trail, the plaza, and on a hero world the regional site of the region you stand in; a reading stone also opens the reading overlay (5.13.1). Marker stones and chapter stones offer no travel. A travel portal opens to the chosen pad. Absent neighbours are not listed.
3. **Random** opens a travel portal to a random card: a plane with probability proportional to the square root of its card count, then a uniform card. Random ignores filters.
4. **Recently visited** is a list of the last 50 stands entered in card view, stored locally. It has no count against a total, no completion state and no reward. It is a bookmark list.

### 6.7 First launch and start screen

1. The start screen shows the title, "You travel as Teferi", the Fan Content Policy notice, the Scryfall credit and an "Enter" control. Loading of the hub happens behind it; "Enter" enables when the hub is ready.
2. The first launch shows a dismissible hint after arrival in the hub: move, run, orbit, interact, map, travel, Esc. Dismissal is remembered. Help reopens it.
3. The visitor arrives in the hub facing the avatar's home-plane portal.

### 6.8 Screenshot

F12 saves a screenshot without HUD to the OS pictures directory and confirms with a transient toast. Every card image in it carries its printed artist line (4.15.5). This is the visitor's share mechanism; there are no URLs.

### 6.9 Settings

1. HDRP quality level (automatic, high, medium, low), resolution scale, frame-rate cap, reduced motion (default follows the OS), five volumes, invert camera axes, gamepad sensitivity, text scale 100 to 150 percent, hint reset, image cache size and clear.
2. Settings persist locally. No accounts, no server, no telemetry.

### 6.10 Accessibility

1. Reduced motion per 5.11, honouring the OS preference by default.
2. Every overlay (map, travel menu, search, settings, help, printings panel) is fully keyboard- and gamepad-operable with visible focus states, built with UI Toolkit.
3. Colour identity and rarity are shown as text in the printings panel and the hover label, so the encoding never carries meaning alone.
4. HUD and panel text meets 4.5:1 contrast against any sky in Appendix C; the HUD draws on a scrim where needed. Text scale 100 to 150 percent.
5. The travel menu, search and the map are the paths to every card without walking (5.9.4), so the full catalogue is reachable without fine motor control.
6. **Screen readers.** Unity 6's accessibility module does not expose desktop UI to macOS VoiceOver or Windows Narrator. The native build therefore has no screen-reader path in v4. The web edition (8.11), which has one (its drawer's list mode reaches every card), stays deployed and is redeployed with every data refresh so it carries the same catalogue as the native build; it is linked from the download page and the help overlay. Open question 11.10 tracks a native path.

### 7.3 Smoothness rules

1. Delta-time motion (5.11.1).
2. No allocations in the per-frame path; pooled stands, labels and audio sources.
3. World streaming is by cluster: clusters within 60 m of the avatar are resident with props and stone geometry; clusters beyond hold stand plinths and swatches only. Images are a separate, smaller radius: 25 m (5.5.1), bounded by slots. The region blend (5.6.3) is baked into the terrain's layer weights at generation, not computed per frame. Streaming never causes a hitch over 4 ms; loads are time-sliced.
4. No level-of-detail transition may pop; stands cross-fade between swatch and image, props fade or dither.
5. Image arrival never causes a visible jump (5.5.5).
6. Camera hand-over on input is continuous in position and velocity.

### 7.4 Reliability

1. A failed data pack file fails loudly at launch with a readable message; the pack is immutable and shipped with the build.
2. A failed image leaves the swatch or the previous image in place; nothing renders as a broken rectangle. Backoff per 4.15.4.
3. If Scryfall is unreachable, every mode, travel, search and filter still works; stands whose images are cached on disk show them; uncached stands show swatches; card view of an uncached card shows the swatch and a one-line notice.
4. The application writes only to: its settings directory and image cache under the OS application-support directory, a versioned terrain cache beside them (`terrain/<dataHash>/<kitVersion>/<slug>.bin`, invalidated by data hash and kit version), and the pictures directory for screenshots.

### 7.5 Security and privacy

1. The only network host is Scryfall's image CDN. No analytics, no error tracking, no update check in v4.
2. External links (Scryfall) open in the system browser.
3. Builds are signed and notarised on macOS and signed on Windows where the owner holds certificates; otherwise the download page documents the unsigned-app steps (open question 11.6).

### 7.6 Maintainability

1. Appendices A, B, C and F, `overrides.json`, `avatar-overrides.json` and the hero volumes files are the only curated data inputs; everything else is derived.
2. The pipeline fails loudly on unknown enum values and on any Appendix C violation (4.12.3).
3. Unity, HDRP and every package are pinned in the project manifest; upgrades are deliberate and reviewed.
4. Everything an engineer changes is text: C#, YAML scenes and prefabs, shader graphs serialised as text, ScriptableObjects, assembly definitions, editor scripts. Binary assets (models, textures, audio) are content supplied under 8.12 and committed through Git LFS; engineers do not author them.

### 7.7 Licence and distribution

1. **Internal evaluation** (the owner and the agents running the build) is not distribution and is not gated. **Distribution** is giving the build to anyone else, friends included, publicly or privately; every distribution waits for the owner's decision in 11.1, and privacy of a distribution is not compliance.
2. When distributed: a free download for macOS (Apple Silicon) and Windows, published as **GitHub Releases on this repository** (`DioPires/eternities-mtg-app`, public since 2026-09-21). One release per tag; each release names the data hash it was built from, and its notes carry the Fan Content Policy notice, the Scryfall credit and the link to the web edition, so the release page plus the README is the download page. An itch.io page is an optional landing page later (11.7). Not Steam (non-goal 6). *Amendment 1 (2026-10-10, owner decision on DEC-950): the first print said the source stayed private and named a separate `eternities-releases` repository; the source is public, so one repository keeps the tag, the built commit, the data hash and the archives together.*

## 8. Architecture

### 8.1 Engine and stack

1. **Unity 6 with HDRP only.** The editor is pinned to `6000.6.5f1` (Unity 6.6) in `unity/Eternities/ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt`; the note at the end of this item says why it is not an LTS. Quality is three HDRP quality levels (high, medium, low) that change resolution scale, shadow and reflection quality, volumetric clouds (high and medium only; a cloud layer on low), volumetric fog quality, water quality and vegetation density. There is **no URP rung in v1**: HDRP and URP are separate render pipelines with separate shaders, materials and features; a URP variant is a second renderer. If HDRP low fails the light Mac ceiling in the slice, Wave 5 decides between a URP build variant (separate materials and substitutions, costed then) and dropping the light Mac as a target (9.5 decision matrix). *Amendment 3 (2026-10-10, owner decision on DEC-951): the first print said Unity 6 LTS. The owner chose the installed `6000.6.5f1`, a tech-stream release (Unity's `SUPPORTED` stream; a line is patched until the next one ships, which the release API shows about every three months: 6.4 on 2026-03-18, 6.5 on 2026-06-15, 6.6 on 2026-08-31), over `6000.3.26f1`, the 6.3 LTS patch released the same day. Each editor upgrade starts by installing the new editor beside the old one on the reference machine (`docs/ci-runner.md` section 5); it is then one reviewed pull request that re-pins `ProjectVersion.txt`, the packages, the README pin table (8.1.2) and the editor lines in `docs/ci-runner.md`, with the four Unity jobs of 8.8.5 green on it; CI and the runner recipe look for exactly the pinned editor under `/Applications/Unity/Hub/Editor/` and fail otherwise. The project moves to an LTS pin only by a decision on DEC-950.*
2. **Systems used, with their nature stated honestly:** Unity Terrain (built in); HDRP Water System (built into HDRP: rivers, lakes, ocean); VFX Graph (package, lava, rain, ash, fireflies); HDRP volumetric fog and clouds (built into HDRP); Cinemachine (package, pinned) for the following camera, the card view camera and portal arrival; the Input System (package) for keyboard, mouse and gamepad; UI Toolkit for every overlay; Addressables (package) for per-world content; standard 3D audio sources with reverb zones (built in); Unity's `CharacterController` as the base of an **own** kinematic avatar controller (the Starter Assets package is a reference only; its Unity 6 HDRP compatibility is not assumed); Animation Rigging (package) and Adobe Mixamo (an external web service, free account) for the shared animation set retargeted onto the humanoid rig. *Amendment 3 (2026-10-10, DEC-951): the exact package versions are the pin table in `unity/Eternities/README.md`, read from `Packages/packages-lock.json` with the `jq` line printed there. HDRP, VFX Graph, Cinemachine, Animation Rigging and the Test Framework ship inside the editor and move with it; the Input System and Addressables come from Unity's registry. That table, not this document, is the source of a version.*
3. **Fallbacks and alternatives, recorded.** Unreal Engine 5 was declined because its Blueprints, materials, landscapes and procedural graphs are binary assets the agent engineers cannot write or review, and macOS is second-class. Godot 4 is the named fallback engine. It is considered only under the single row of the 9.5 decision matrix that names it, after bottleneck attribution shows the reference machine's miss lies in HDRP's fixed costs rather than in our content or code. A delivery miss, an artistic miss, or a light-Mac miss never invokes it, because Godot would rebuild the same controller, streaming and content integrations. three.js is withdrawn for this scope (8.11).

### 8.2 Repository layout

### 8.4 Scene structure and rendering

1. One persistent scene holds systems (data, audio, UI, avatar, camera rigs, streaming) and the HUD. The hub, each kit world and each hero scene are loaded through Addressables behind the portal transition.
2. A world is: a terrain generated from the kit and the theme row at load (cached per world on disk, 7.4.4), a water configuration placed only in the water the region rows name (Appendix F.1; the theme row's on a kit world) and in the kit's water zones (the world minus the 3 m water buffer around paths, corridors, aprons, pads and stones, 5.4.1; water crosses the trail or a site path only through a ford or bridge prefab), a prop scatter (seeded, instanced, run **after** the pack is read, following each region's kit and variant, and excluding stands, aprons, corridors, the trail, site paths, pads, stones, hero volumes and the 1 m prop clearance around them), the hero scene if any, the stands, waystones and marker stones, the return portal, the sky and lighting rig, reverb zones and audio emitters. The following camera is a Cinemachine rig with a collider that pulls in on props; the 1 m clearance along paths and corridors is what keeps that rare.
3. **Stand images.** Per world one `Texture2DArray` of **384 slots** at 244 × 340 (the `normal` image downscaled by exactly one half, aspect preserved, no padding), RGBA8 with a full mip chain, trilinear sampling, so a stand 25 m away is minified without shimmer. 384 × 332 KB × 1.33 is about 170 MB; with card view's two uncompressed `large` textures (about 5 MB) the resident total is about 175 MB, inside the 192 MB target and the 256 MB ceiling. Decoding and downscaling happen off the main thread; the upload is one `Texture2D` to array-slice copy per image, time-sliced to at most two uploads per frame. No runtime texture compression in v1 (`Texture2D.Compress` yields DXT1 for RGB and DXT5 for RGBA input, is uninterruptible per call, and would change the pixels the colour test compares); it is an optimisation to revisit if GPU memory, not frame time, is the constraint (11.14). Residency: the loader fills slots nearest-first within 25 m and evicts the least-recently-near slot when a nearer stand needs one; the slot count is the hard bound and the 25 m radius is a preference (5.5.5). A world transition releases the old world's array before the new one allocates.
4. **The isolated image path.** Image surfaces (stands in the world and the card in card view) must be opaque to the world's rendering (so fog, exposure, depth-of-field and ambient occlusion treat them as solid objects and nothing renders behind them) and must reach the screen with their exact pixels (no lighting, exposure, fog, post-processing or temporal anti-aliasing). Two renders with matching, unjittered, output-resolution buffers achieve this:
   - **In the main camera**, every image surface is drawn in the opaque pass as an ordinary opaque object with a flat material in the stand's swatch colour. It writes depth and colour like any object, so occlusion, depth-of-field, fog and exposure are all consistent and the world behind it is correctly hidden. Its colour is a placeholder that the composite below replaces.
   - **An exhibit camera**, slaved to the main camera's pose and field of view, renders at output resolution with the **unjittered** projection and with post-processing, anti-aliasing and dynamic resolution disabled. It renders two things into its own targets: an **occluder depth** prepass of the world's opaque geometry within 60 m (terrain, props, hero geometry, avatar, plinths), and then the image surfaces with their exact texels (unlit, exposure-independent, fog-free), depth-tested against that occluder depth, into an RGBA **exhibit buffer** whose alpha is the image surfaces' **complete** coverage: the whole card rectangle as projected, not an inset. The composite must replace every pixel of the main camera's placeholder and lose no pixel of the card; whether the boundary is clean under temporal anti-aliasing, depth-of-field and resolution scaling is not assumed from the independent buffers but **measured** by test (f) below, and row I of the slice gates on it.
   - **Composite.** After the main camera's post-processing and upscaling, a final pass blends the exhibit buffer over the main output where its alpha is set. The exhibit camera's buffers are at output resolution and unjittered, so there is no mismatch with the main camera's jittered or upscaled depth; occlusion is decided by the exhibit camera's own occluder depth; TAA never sees the exhibit pixels; depth-of-field blurs the world (which is in the main camera's colour) and not the card (which is composited after).
   - **Cost and alternative.** The occluder prepass is a second depth-only draw of nearby opaque geometry; the slice measures it. If it costs more than 1.5 ms on the reference machine, the alternative is an HDRP custom pass at the after-post-processing injection point drawing the image surfaces with the unjittered projection against a depth buffer the pass itself re-renders unjittered at output resolution (HDRP documents that the camera's own depth at that point is jittered and may be at render resolution, so it must not be reused); the composite and the opaque placeholder draw stay the same. Row I of the slice's acceptance matrix (9.5) gates both.
   - **Tests** on the pinned HDRP version: (a) colour isolation: a known card rendered at exactly 244 × 340 screen pixels under a bright sky, a dark sky and dense fog, compared to the source image downscaled by the same half-step filter, per-channel tolerance 1 on 255; (b) occlusion: an occluder box placed between camera and stand hides exactly the pixels the main camera hides, to within the 0.5 px inset; (c) motion: twenty frames of camera motion with TAA on produce no frame-to-frame change on a static image surface beyond the tolerance; (d) depth-of-field on in card view leaves the card pixels within tolerance while the world behind it measurably blurs; (e) resolution scale 0.75 keeps (a) within tolerance 2 on 255; (f) boundary: along the entire perimeter of a composited card, with TAA on, with depth-of-field on, and at resolution scales 1.0 and 0.75, no pixel of the swatch placeholder survives and no pixel of the card is lost, to within one output pixel of the exact projected edge, for a static camera and across twenty frames of camera motion. The slice verifies (a) to (f) before anything else is built on the path.
5. **Terrain.** Unity Terrain per world, heightmap resolution from the world radius (513 for worlds under 100 m, 1,025 to 260 m, 2,049 beyond), layers from the region's kit and variant with a 20 m blend across region boundaries written into the layer weights at generation (5.6.3), generated by the kit's noise with the seed, flattened under the trail, site paths, corridors, pads, stones, aprons and the plaza, and cached (7.4.4).

### F.1 `regions.json`

```json
{
  "plane": "dominaria",
  "regions": [
    {
      "id": "shiv",
      "name": "Shiv",
      "kit": "volcanic",
      "variant": "basalt-steppe",
      "anchor": [0.7, 0.2],
      "weight": 1.0,
      "water": "lava",
      "ambient": "volcanic",
      "sentence": {"text": "<one sentence on the place, in our words>", "source": "https://magic.wizards.com/en/planes/dominaria"},
      "set_piece": "a proposal for the owner's landmark in this region",
      "sources": ["https://magic.wizards.com/en/planes/dominaria"]
    }
  ]
}
```

The row names places and weights, never positions in metres: the allocator places (5.4.5, 5.4.6). The anchor is a direction and a rough distance from the plaza inside the unit disc; the weight grows or shrinks the region against its neighbours. A region may hold no site: it is then scenery the trail crosses, and the map names it without a travel link (6.3.4).

### F.2 `eras.json`

```json
{
  "plane": "dominaria",
  "kind": "lore",
  "accepted": {"by": "owner", "on": "2026-10-20", "issue": "DEC-9xx", "digest": "sha256:<64 hex>"},
  "eras": [
    {
      "id": "brothers-war",
      "name": "The Brothers' War",
      "years": "<the era's place in the plane's history, in words>",
      "region": "argoth",
      "sets": ["atq", "bro", "brc"],
      "headline": [],
      "paragraphs": [
        [
          {"text": "<sentence>", "source": "https://magic.wizards.com/en/story/..."},
          {"text": "<sentence>", "source": "printing:<scryfall_id>#front"}
        ]
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

**Acceptance and its digest.** `digest` is `sha256:` followed by the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON (sorted keys, no whitespace, UTF-8) of the three files concatenated in the order regions, eras, keywords, with the `accepted` object removed from `eras.json` before hashing. The owner's acceptance names the digest on the tables' issue; the pipeline recomputes it on every run and a mismatch means the tables changed after acceptance: the `accepted` record is reported as **stale**, a development build proceeds, and a distribution build fails naming the entry (4.16.5). Any edit therefore needs re-acceptance.

**Product tables (the Blind Eternities).** With `kind: product`, a region is a **set family** (Commander decks, Masters and reprint sets, supplemental and silver-bordered sets, promotional and box-set printings, and so on) carrying its `sets` list, and an era is **one family over one release period**: its `region` is the family's region and its `sets` lie in that region's list, so every card stands in its family's region and the trail runs through the families in the order the table gives. The era ceiling is 24 for a product table. The text describes the products that printed the cards, in our words, from Scryfall's and Wizards' set pages (5.13.8).

### F.3 `keywords.json`

```json
{"plane": "dominaria", "keywords": [{"word": "Shivan", "region": "shiv"}, {"word": "Tolarian", "region": "tolaria"}]}
```

`word` is matched as a whole word on any face name, case-insensitive and Unicode case-folded (5.4.3); table order is precedence; a word maps to exactly one region. The report lists every match and every card that matches words of two regions. On dataset `a5846bec28d1447e`, 326 of Dominaria's cards carry one of 24 such words (Tolarian, Shivan, Urborg, Benalish, Llanowar, Keldon, Yavimaya, Phyrexian, Otaria, Vesuva, Zhalfir, Jamuraa, Thran, Argoth, Koilos, Serra, Academy, Femeref, Kjeldor, Balduvia, Suq'Ata, Sarpadia, Icatia, Ice Age; counted on Scryfall on 2026-10-12). The drafter chooses the words; the report shows what they move.

### F.4 Sources

A `source` is a **locator** of one of five forms, and nothing else validates:

| Form | What it points at | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| `https://magic.wizards.com/<path>` | a story, a plane page or a set page | the parsed host is exactly `magic.wizards.com`; scheme `https` |
| `https://scryfall.com/<path>` | a card or set page | parsed host exactly `scryfall.com` |
| `https://mtg.wiki/<path>` | an article | parsed host exactly `mtg.wiki` |
| `card:<oracle_id>` | the card's name and type line | the id is in the dataset |
| `printing:<scryfall_id>#front` or `#back` | the printed flavour text of that face of that printing | the printing is in the dataset and the face exists |

A host is validated by parsing the URL, never by a substring match. **What a source is.** A source supports a sentence: the reviewer reads the source and confirms the sentence's facts are there. A source is never copied: the MTG Wiki's text is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (read 2026-10-12) and would bind ours, and Wizards' story text is theirs. The reviewer therefore checks two things per sentence, **support** and **independent wording**, and the verdict records for each sentence the passage relied on (a short quotation inside the review, which is review material, never product text) and that the sentence is not that passage reworded. One source per sentence is bookkeeping that makes the check possible and shows the visitor where to read more; it is not a licence determination, and the product's classification stays the owner's gate (11.1). The visitor sees every source of an era's sentences and of its region's sentence in the reading stone's fold (5.13.1).
